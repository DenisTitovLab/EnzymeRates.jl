EnzymeRates.Derivation.compile_rate_equation — Method
compile_rate_equation(m) -> rate

The rate law of mechanism m as a function: rate(concs, params, Keq) returns the net reaction rate per unit enzyme at the metabolite concentrations concs and the constants params (both NamedTuples), with the equilibrium constant Keq. Multiply by the enzyme concentration for an absolute rate. The law is derived from the mechanism's elementary steps by the King–Altman/Cha method, so one call gives the turnover of the whole mechanism, not the rate of one step.

params holds the constants a fit estimates, parameters(rate), the same names as parameters(m), and concs the concentrations of metabolites(rate); both are tuples of Symbols. The law computes each dependent constant from params and Keq by its Haldane or Wegscheider relation. Both NamedTuples are read by name, so their fields may come in any order, and fields the law does not read are ignored.

The first call derives the law and compiles it to straight-line arithmetic, once per mechanism and session; every later call is fast and allocates nothing, so compile once, outside a loop. Equal mechanisms give the same rate. Calling rate errors when the rate law of m is not defined (check_valid_rate_equation), and with a RateEquationTooLargeError when its term count (rate_equation_term_count) exceeds the term cap. rate_equation_string prints the law.

julia> using EnzymeRates

julia> m = @enzyme_mechanism begin
           substrates: S
           products: P
           steps: begin
               E + S ⇌ ES
               ES <--> EP
               EP ⇌ E + P
           end
       end;

julia> rate = compile_rate_equation(m)
compiled rate equation

julia> parameters(rate)
(:K_EP_to_E_P, :K_ES_to_E_S, :k_ES_to_EP)

julia> params = (K_EP_to_E_P = 1.0, K_ES_to_E_S = 2.0, k_ES_to_EP = 3.0);

julia> rate((S = 1.0, P = 0.5), params, 4.0)
0.65625

EnzymeRates.Derivation.dependent_parameter_values — Method
dependent_parameter_values(m, params::NamedTuple, Keq::Real) -> NamedTuple

The values of the dependent constants of m's rate law, by name in name order, each computed from the fitted constants in params and the equilibrium constant Keq by its Haldane or Wegscheider relation (the relations rate_equation_string prints). params holds the values of parameters(m) by name; other names are ignored. An error names every constant of parameters(m) that params lacks. merge(params, dependent_parameter_values(m, params, Keq)) is every constant.

julia> using EnzymeRates

julia> m = @enzyme_mechanism begin
           substrates: S
           products: P

               E + S ⇌ ES

julia> p = (K_EP_to_E_P = 1.0, K_ES_to_E_S = 2.0, k_ES_to_EP = 3.0);

julia> dependent_parameter_values(m, p, 4.0)

EnzymeRates.Fitting.fit_rate_equation — Method
fit_rate_equation(fp::FittingProblem, optimizer;
    n_restarts=20, maxtime=60.0, maxiters=10_000_000,
    abstol=nothing, reltol=nothing, callback=nothing,
    lb=centre .- 15.0, ub=centre .+ 15.0,
    solver_kwargs=(;))

Fit rate constants by minimizing loss! using Optimization.jl.

Runs n_restarts independent optimizations from random initial points and returns the best result. Each start draws every log-space parameter from a normal distribution around its centre, with a standard deviation of 2, and clamps it to the box. The centre comes from the geometric means of the table's nonzero concentrations and of its |Rate|: a binding's K centres on the concentrations it binds, any other rapid-equilibrium K on what the step gives off over what it takes up, a rate constant on the rate over the concentrations it takes up, a regulator's K on its ligand's concentrations and L on 1.

maxtime and maxiters are Optimization.jl common solver options forwarded to every Optimization.solve call. abstol, reltol, and callback are common options forwarded only when set (otherwise each solver uses its own default). solver_kwargs is a NamedTuple of solver-specific options forwarded verbatim to solve (e.g. (; popsize=200) for a CMA-ES solver that supports it). A key present in both a named common option and solver_kwargs takes the solver_kwargs value. lb/ub are the log-space bounds passed to the OptimizationProblem. They hold absolute log values, and by default lie 15 log units either side of each centre, so the box follows the data.

In relative mode the fit multiplies the returned rate constants by one factor (Normalized vs absolute rate). With reference anchors the factor is the geometric mean of measured over predicted turnover at them; without, it is 1 over the largest absolute rate predicted over the data rows. A fit for which no such factor exists returns its parameters unscaled, with loss = Inf and retcode = :ReferenceFailed, and does not throw. In absolute mode, and when no restart reaches a finite loss (loss = Inf, retcode = :NoFiniteLoss), it returns the rate constants as fitted.

Returns a NamedTuple (params, loss, retcode, at_bound) where:

  • params: the fitted constants, by the names of parameters(fp.rate), as a NamedTuple
  • loss: the best loss value achieved
  • retcode: the Symbol form of the best restart's sol.retcode. Only :Success indicates the optimizer converged on its own criteria; any other value (e.g. :MaxTime — hit the time budget; :Failure; :Default — ran but did not flag success) means the fit should be treated as un-converged (check retcode !== :Success); :ReferenceFailed marks a failed reference scale.
  • at_bound: the names of the fitted parameters whose best point lies within 0.01 log units of its lb or ub, read before the relative-mode scale, which can move a reported rate constant outside the box. () when no restart reaches a finite loss. "At bound" does not mean wrong: a parameter no data constrain wanders across the box.

EnzymeRates.identify_rate_equation — Method
identify_rate_equation(prob; optimizer,
    min_beam_width=50, loss_rel_threshold=1.3, loss_abs_threshold=0.001,
    loss_parsimony_threshold=0.99,
    max_param_count=20, eq_complexity_filter=337,
    optional_allosteric_regulators=Symbol[],
    optional_competitive_inhibitors=Symbol[],
    n_restarts=20, maxtime=600.0, maxiters=10_000_000,
    abstol=nothing, reltol=nothing, callback=nothing, solver_kwargs=(;),
    n_cv_candidates=5, se_threshold=1.0,
    save_dir=_default_save_dir(), show_progress=true)

Find the best rate equation for the reaction and data of prob and return an IdentifyRateEquationResults. A beam search fits candidate mechanisms in order of parameter count and expands the mechanisms whose rate-law key enters its beam. A key enters when its loss is at most the loss cutoff or when it ranks within the min_beam_width floor. The cutoff is the first of the two bounds below, or the smaller of the two once a smaller parameter count has a fitted loss. Leave-one-group-out cross-validation then selects one mechanism by the one-standard-error rule. Best mechanism selection states both rules and the files the search writes to save_dir.

Keyword Arguments

  • optimizer: Optimization.jl optimizer (required). Recommended: CMAEvolutionStrategyOpt() from OptimizationCMAEvolutionStrategy.
  • min_beam_width::Int = 50: the floor, the fewest rate-law keys the beam takes at each parameter count over the whole search, lowest loss first
  • loss_rel_threshold::Float64 = 1.3: the first bound of the loss cutoff at a parameter count is this factor times the best loss there, plus loss_abs_threshold
  • loss_abs_threshold::Float64 = 0.001: the additive term of the first bound
  • loss_parsimony_threshold::Float64 = 0.99: the second bound is this factor times the best loss at any smaller parameter count; Inf disables it
  • max_param_count::Int = 20: the search proposes no mechanism with more fitted constants than this (parameters)
  • eq_complexity_filter::Int = 337: the most terms a proposed rate law may have (EnzymeRates.Derivation.rate_equation_term_count). The count is roughly the number of terms in the law's denominator, both conformations counted for an allosteric mechanism. The default admits a fully steady-state random-order bi-bi (336 terms); denser laws are slow to derive and fit. Counting costs no derivation.
  • optional_allosteric_regulators::Vector{Symbol} = Symbol[]: declared allosteric regulators the seed may omit. A reaction that declares regulators seeds the beam fully regulated by default; naming a regulator here lets the beam also start from seeds that do not bind it (added back later by expansion). Listing every declared regulator restores the unregulated seeds.
  • optional_competitive_inhibitors::Vector{Symbol} = Symbol[]: declared competitive inhibitors the seed may omit, as above.
  • n_restarts::Int = 20: multi-start restarts per fit
  • maxtime::Real = 600.0: max time per optimizer run in seconds (common solver option, forwarded to Optimization.solve); each of a fit's n_restarts runs gets it, so a fit can take up to n_restarts × maxtime
  • maxiters::Integer = 10_000_000: max iterations per optimizer run (common solver option, forwarded to Optimization.solve)
  • abstol/reltol/callback = nothing: Optimization.jl common solver options, forwarded to Optimization.solve only when set
  • solver_kwargs::NamedTuple = (;): solver-specific options forwarded verbatim to Optimization.solve (e.g. (; popsize=200) for a CMA-ES solver that supports it); the caller matches its contents to optimizer
  • n_cv_candidates::Int = 5: how many distinct rate-law keys at each parameter count, those of lowest loss, enter cross-validation (at least 1)
  • se_threshold::Float64 = 1.0: the multiplier of the one-standard-error rule; larger values favor simpler equations
  • save_dir::String: the directory the search writes its CSVs to, by default a new yyyy_mm_dd_results directory in the working directory
  • show_progress::Bool = true: print progress lines and append them to save_dir/progress.log

Errors when

  • n_cv_candidates is less than 1;
  • save_dir already holds a .csv file or a progress.log, so that the results of two runs never mix;
  • the starting mechanisms cannot be built:
    • the reaction's metabolite names spell one string two ways (such as Ac, CoA and AcCoA);
    • the reaction has no catalytic cycle: every route needs an isomerization converting more than three substrates, or more than three products counting a covalent residue as one;
    • no mechanism binds every required regulator;
  • no mechanism is fitted to a finite loss (when every starting mechanism throws, the error quotes the first failure);
  • a cross-validation fold's loss is not finite;
  • an internal check fails, such as two mechanisms whose texts share a mechanism_id.

EnzymeRates.Derivation.parameters — Method
parameters(m) -> Tuple{Vararg{Symbol}}
parameters(rate) -> Tuple{Vararg{Symbol}}

The names of the constants of the rate law of m that a fit estimates, as a tuple of Symbols: the independent constants in name order, then an allosteric mechanism's regulator constants and L. These are the names params holds in a call of compile_rate_equation(m), whose result rate gives the same names as a compile-time constant. The equilibrium constant Keq is not among them, since it is given, never fitted. The rate law computes every other constant from these and Keq (see dependent_parameter_values). Errors when the rate law of m is not defined (check_valid_rate_equation).

EnzymeRates.Plotting.plot_rate_equation_on_data — Method
plot_rate_equation_on_data(m, params, table, Keq; absolute = false,
    concentration_unit = "M", columns = 5, figure = (;), axis = (;)) -> Makie.Figure
plot_rate_equation_on_data(rate::Function, params, table; absolute = false,
    concentration_unit = "M", columns = 5, figure = (;), axis = (;)) -> Makie.Figure

Draws the rate equation of the mechanism m, with the equilibrium constant Keq, or a rate function rate of your own, at params over the measured rates of table, one panel per group in order of first appearance, columns panels per row, and returns the Makie.Figure. It fits nothing. It needs a Makie backend: run using CairoMakie (or another backend) first.

using EnzymeRates, CairoMakie
fig = plot_rate_equation_on_data(m, fit.params, data, 2.0)
save("fit.pdf", fig)

michaelis_menten(concentrations, params) =
    params.Vmax * concentrations.S / (params.Km + concentrations.S)
fig = plot_rate_equation_on_data(michaelis_menten, (Vmax = 2.0, Km = 1e-4), data)

Arguments

  • m: a mechanism, which draws its rate law, compile_rate_equation(m)(concentrations, params, Keq); or rate, a function, which draws rate(concentrations, params). concentrations is a NamedTuple of one row's concentrations, one per concentration column of table.
  • params: a NamedTuple. With a mechanism, it holds every name of parameters(m), as a fit's params does; other names are ignored. A function receives it as given.
  • table: a Tables.jl table, such as a DataFrame, a NamedTuple of columns or the result of CSV.File, with a group column, a Rate column and concentration columns. Every other column is a concentration, so remove columns that hold anything else, such as a pH or a source name. A mechanism needs a column for each of its metabolites and ignores the concentrations of metabolites it does not bind; the panels still show them, so that measurements differing only in one of them are not drawn as one series. The table must pass the checks of FittingProblem.
  • Keq: the reaction's equilibrium constant, with a mechanism; a rate function holds its own.
  • absolute: false for rates relative within each group, true for absolute per-enzyme turnover rates.
  • concentration_unit: the unit of the table's concentrations. With "M", each x axis shows M, mM, µM or nM to fit its largest value, and each legend value picks its own prefix. Any other unit is shown as given, and the numbers are not converted.
  • columns: panels per row.
  • figure: keywords for Makie.Figure, such as (; size = (510, 330), fontsize = 7.5). Every text size follows fontsize (10 by default); the default size is 320 × 200 per panel.
  • axis: keywords for every panel's Makie.Axis, such as (; xgridvisible = true).

Values in figure and axis override the built-in defaults.

A panel

The x axis is the metabolite with the most distinct concentrations in the group; a tie goes to the earlier one in metabolites(m), then to the earlier column of table. Each distinct combination of the other metabolites' concentrations is a series with its own colour and marker: the measured Rate as points and the rate equation as a curve, a mechanism's per unit enzyme. Where the rate equation does not respond to a metabolite, the curve along it is flat, and series that differ only in it share a curve. With absolute = false, each group's curves are multiplied by one factor, the geometric mean of |Rate / predicted| over the group's rows; no parameter changes. Beside the panel stand the metabolites held at one nonzero concentration, then a legend headed by those that vary between series.

The y axis, in the table's units of Rate, includes zero and shows curves up to 1.6 times the largest rate. When a group's largest absolute rate is below 0.01, the axis counts in a power of ten, noted in the panel's corner. The x axis is linear from zero unless axis sets xscale, such as log10; then each curve runs from the group's smallest x to its largest.

Errors

  • ErrorException when no Makie backend is loaded, or when params lacks a fitted parameter of the mechanism.
  • ArgumentError when columns is less than 1.
  • ArgumentError when axis sets an xscale other than identity and a group has an x of zero, which a log axis cannot show.
  • The errors FittingProblem raises for a table it rejects, with every column but group and Rate checked as a concentration.

EnzymeRates.Derivation.rate_equation_string — Method
rate_equation_string(m; coefficients = false, coefficient_values = true) -> String

Return the rate law of mechanism m as a multi-line String (it returns the text; it does not print).

The text runs as Julia code once params, concs and Keq are bound, as they are in a call of compile_rate_equation(m). It destructures parameters(m) from params and the metabolites from concs, and reads the equilibrium constant Keq as an input of its own. It lists each dependent constant under # Wegscheider constraints: or # Haldane constraints: as a reference line in Keq and the independent constants. It ends with v = (Q) * (H) / (D), the rate per unit enzyme, in the independent constants. H = Π[S] − Π[P]/Keq vanishes at equilibrium; Q and D have positive terms. Numerator and denominator are divided by a normalizer that makes every term of D dimensionless, when one exists, chosen by the rules of Normalization; a law with none prints as derived, and its terms keep their units. A factor of the normalizer that is not a single term, or an integer to a fractional power, prints once as N = …, after the reference lines. An allosteric mechanism prints each conformation's numerator Q_A, Q_I, denominator D_A, D_I, normalizer N_A, N_I, free-enzyme weight F_A, F_I (when they differ) and regulatory-site factor W_A, W_I, then the MWC combination.

With coefficients = true the law reads in named coefficients, v = n_0 * (H) / (1 + d_S * S + …), each named by the concentrations it multiplies (d_A²_B for A^2 * B). That text runs as Julia code too. It destructures params and concs, then prints the coefficient comment, N (N_A, N_I) when the normalizer prints, and each coefficient's value in the fitted constants, one line each. For an allosteric law it prints Q_c, D_c and F_c in those coefficients and the site factors W_c. It ends with the v line; the reference lines print only in the full law.

coefficient_values = false (with coefficients = true; ArgumentError otherwise) leaves out everything that defines a coefficient: the params line, the comment, N and each coefficient's value. What remains is the concs line, for an allosteric law the Q_c, D_c, F_c and W_c lines, and the v line. This text names coefficients it does not define, so it does not run; it is the readable form the search CSVs hold.

Errors when the rate law of m is not defined (check_valid_rate_equation), and with a RateEquationTooLargeError when its term count (rate_equation_term_count) exceeds the term cap.

julia> using EnzymeRates

julia> m = @enzyme_mechanism begin
           substrates: S
           products: P
           steps: begin
               E + S ⇌ ES
               ES <--> EP
               EP ⇌ E + P
           end
       end;

julia> print(rate_equation_string(m))
(; K_EP_to_E_P, K_ES_to_E_S, k_ES_to_EP) = params
(; S, P) = concs
# Haldane constraints:
k_EP_to_ES = K_EP_to_E_P * k_ES_to_EP / (K_ES_to_E_S * Keq)
v = (k_ES_to_EP / K_ES_to_E_S) * (S - P / Keq) / (1 + S / K_ES_to_E_S + P / K_EP_to_E_P)

julia> print(rate_equation_string(m; coefficients = true))
(; K_EP_to_E_P, K_ES_to_E_S, k_ES_to_EP) = params
(; S, P) = concs
# The coefficients are functions of the fitted constants, not parameters:
n_0 = k_ES_to_EP / K_ES_to_E_S
d_S = 1 / K_ES_to_E_S
d_P = 1 / K_EP_to_E_P
v = n_0 * (S - P / Keq) / (1 + d_S * S + d_P * P)

julia> print(rate_equation_string(m; coefficients = true, coefficient_values = false))
(; S, P) = concs
v = n_0 * (S - P / Keq) / (1 + d_S * S + d_P * P)

EnzymeRates.Fitting.test_rate_equation — Method
test_rate_equation(m, params::NamedTuple, table, Keq::Real; absolute = false) -> Float64

The fitting loss of mechanism m at the constants params on table, with the equilibrium constant Keq: the loss fit_rate_equation minimizes, for scoring a fit on data it did not see. params must hold every name of parameters(m), as a fit's params does; other names are ignored. table has the columns FittingProblem needs. EnzymeRates.Derivation.RateEquationTooLargeError — Type
RateEquationTooLargeError <: Exception

Thrown for a mechanism whose rate law has more terms (rate_equation_term_count) than the term cap, the most the package derives: a law that large takes too long to compile and is unlikely to be useful for fitting. Calling the law that compile_rate_equation returns throws it, as do rate_equation_string and rate_equation_dedup_key. To avoid it, check the term count, which derives nothing, before asking for the law; identify_rate_equation proposes no law with more terms than its eq_complexity_filter. The error has no fields; its message states the cap. The type is public, not exported: catch it as EnzymeRates.Derivation.RateEquationTooLargeError.

EnzymeRates.DSL.@enzyme_mechanism — Macro
@enzyme_mechanism begin
    substrates: S, ...
    products: P, ...
    modifiers: I, ...
    oligomeric_state: 2              # an allosteric mechanism only
    steps: begin
        E + S ⇌ ES                   # :: EqualAI on every statement, if allosteric
        ...
    end
    regulatory_site(2): X::OnlyA     # an allosteric mechanism only
end

Build a mechanism from its elementary steps. The value is a Mechanism, which compile_rate_equation turns into a rate law that evaluates without allocating. Two writings of one mechanism give equal values. It prints as the block that builds it, each list sorted and one line per kinetic group in canonical order.

Labels:

  • substrates: and products: (required) name the reactants, without atoms; atoms belong in @enzyme_reaction.
  • modifiers: names the metabolites that bind the catalytic site without reacting, such as inhibitors and activators. A name is declared once: a substrate or product that also binds where it does not react is not listed here, since a step that binds it simply takes it up (ES + S ⇌ ESS).
  • steps: (required) holds the steps in one begin ... end block.
  • substrates:, products: and modifiers: may repeat; their names accumulate.
  • oligomeric_state: and regulatory_site(n): belong to an allosteric mechanism (below).

Steps, one per line:

  • lhs ⇌ rhs is a rapid-equilibrium step and lhs <--> rhs a steady-state one; either direction may be written, but the isomerizations of one kinetic group share their constants in the direction each is written, so write them the same way round.
  • Each side holds one enzyme form and the metabolites the step takes up (left) or gives off (right): E + S ⇌ ES, EA + B <--> EQ + P. A name on a side that no label declares is an enzyme form, and each side holds exactly one.
  • An enzyme form's name is any identifier that names no metabolite, such as ES, EAB or Fstar; E is the free enzyme. The name says nothing to the package, but it is part of its constants' names, so a name that tells what the form holds keeps the rate law readable: the mechanism search writes EAB for E with A and B bound, and E_res_plusA_minusP for a ping-pong's covalent intermediate after A bound and P left.
  • A name is one form wherever it is written, so a misspelt name is another form: EI + S ⇌ EIS beside ES + I ⇌ ESI makes EIS and ESI two forms. The constructor rejects a form that no step joins to E, and the guard some other slips, such as a name that leaves no cycle turning substrates into products, or a step that forgets a metabolite (ESI <--> ES for ESI <--> ES + I). Others pass and write another mechanism.
  • A parenthesized tuple of steps is one kinetic group, whose steps share their constants: (E + I ⇌ EI, ES + I ⇌ EIS).
  • A substrate or product binds wherever a step takes it up. Substrate inhibition is ES + S ⇌ ESS; the form's name tells the two bindings apart.

Every mechanism holds the free enzyme E, and its steps join every form to it. Each declared name takes part in some step: a step takes up or gives off each substrate and product, and binds each modifier.

Constants are named after the forms and metabolites a step joins: K_ES_to_E_S for E + S ⇌ ES, k_ES_to_EP and k_EP_to_ES for ES <--> EP, K_ESSmod_to_ES_S for ES + S ⇌ ESSmod.

An allosteric (MWC) mechanism: the enzyme is active (A) or inactive (I), the free enzyme's inactive-to-active ratio is L, and allosteric regulators bind regulatory sites apart from the catalytic one. A mechanism is allosteric when its statements carry allosteric states: every statement of steps: ends with one, a step after its right-hand side, a parenthesized group after its closing parenthesis, and (step,) :: State for a group of one. Some statements with a state and others without is an error. Only an allosteric mechanism takes:

  • oligomeric_state: (default 1), the number of catalytic subunits, which change conformation together.
  • regulatory_site(n): X::State, Y::State, one regulatory site, present n times, and its ligands in any order, each with its state. A ligand sits on one site line. It binds only its regulatory site, unless modifiers: lists it too, which lets it bind the catalytic site as well.

A state says how a statement's constants, or a ligand's binding, differ between the conformations: EqualAI shares one constant, NonequalAI takes one for each (K_A_… and K_I_…), OnlyA acts in the active conformation only and OnlyI in the inactive one only. A catalytic statement or a ligand takes any of the four, as long as the active conformation still turns substrate into product. The conformations interconvert only at free E, so a conformation that lacks a statement also lacks every form its other statements no longer join to free E, and the steps between such forms, whatever their state: with E + S ⇌ ES :: OnlyA and EP ⇌ E + P :: OnlyA, the inactive conformation has neither ES nor EP, and ES <--> EP does not run in it.

When the macro's code runs, it rejects a mechanism whose rate law is not defined where data sit, with an error that states the fault and names the steps at fault where it has them, when:

  • a cycle of steps takes up or gives off a metabolite in another proportion than the reaction, as when a step forgets one;
  • no cycle of steps turns substrates into products;
  • a cycle of rapid-equilibrium steps does, so the rate has no finite value;
  • with every product, every substrate, one reactant or every modifier absent, the steady state is not unique, or is not the limit the rate law gives as the rapid-equilibrium steps speed up, or the law has no value (0/0).

A step with a state is switched off in the conformation that lacks it, and it keeps its kind, rapid equilibrium or steady state, in both. The steps that conformation keeps join the forms into islands; every island but free E's lies higher in energy and holds no enzyme. A switched-off step between two islands loses its rate uphill and keeps its rate downhill, or, at steady state, may stop. One inside an island stops at steady state; at rapid equilibrium it cannot be switched off, and the macro rejects it. The macro also rejects the states when it finds no choice of the islands' energies under which it can show that the rate law is the limit. It rejects them too when they allow more choices than it searches and it can show the limit under none of the three it tries first. How an excluded step is switched off gives the rules.

EnzymeRates.DSL.@enzyme_reaction — Macro
@enzyme_reaction begin
    substrates: S[C6H12O6], ...
    products: P[C6H13O9P], ...
    competitive_inhibitors: I, ...
    allosteric_regulators: A, ...
    oligomeric_state: 2
    shared_catalytic_site: (S, P), ...
end

Build the EnzymeReaction that the mechanism search starts from: its reactants with their atoms, and the regulators and oligomeric states the search may use.

  • substrates: and products: (required) list each reactant with its atoms, one chemical formula in brackets: S[C6H12O6]. The atoms of the two sides balance, and a name is a substrate or a product, not both.
  • competitive_inhibitors: lists the competitive inhibitors, names that bind the catalytic site without reacting. A substrate or product listed here may also bind where it does not react, as an inhibitor.
  • allosteric_regulators: lists the allosteric regulators, names that bind regulatory sites. A type tag, A::Activator or A::Inhibitor, pins the regulator to the allosteric state that activates or inhibits; an untagged one (:unspecified) may take either. Only these entries take a type tag. A name may be both a competitive inhibitor and an allosteric regulator.
  • oligomeric_state: gives the oligomeric states the search may assign: one positive Int, or a tuple of distinct ones such as (1, 2, 4). It defaults to 1 and is given at most once.
  • shared_catalytic_site: lists (substrate, product) pairs, in either order, that bind one catalytic site, so never both at once.
  • The list labels may repeat; their entries accumulate.

No metabolite is named Keq, L, N, params, concs or v, or holds _: a rate equation keeps those names, and its constant names join names with _. Nor is one named E, the name of the free enzyme form; a name is an identifier that is no keyword. The mechanism search names an enzyme form by joining its bound metabolites' names without a separator, so the names may not spell one string two ways: with Ac, CoA and AcCoA, E with Ac and CoA bound and E with AcCoA bound would both be EAcCoA. The search rejects such a reaction when it starts and names both spellings (Ac + CoA and AcCoA both spell AcCoA); rename one (Acetyl). A reactant bound where it does not react is written Xmod in a form's name, which counts as a name too.

EnzymeRates.Mechanisms.EnzymeReaction — Type
EnzymeReaction

A reaction: its substrates, products, optional regulators, and the oligomeric states the mechanism enumerator may consider. It is the entry point to the package — pair it with rate data in an IdentifyRateEquationProblem, or pass it to EnzymeRates.Enumeration.candidate_mechanisms to enumerate candidate mechanisms.

Fields

  • substrates::Vector{Reactant} and products::Vector{Reactant} — each reactant carrying its per-atom inventory.
  • regulators::Vector{Regulator} — competitive inhibitors and allosteric regulators, each with its kind and effect.
  • allowed_oligomeric_states::Vector{Int} — the oligomeric states, one or more, that the allosteric enumerator may assign to the catalytic core.
  • shared_catalytic_site::Vector{Tuple{Symbol,Symbol}} — (substrate, product) pairs that bind the same catalytic site, and so are never both bound to it at once.

Construct one with the @enzyme_reaction DSL. Each reactant takes an atom bracket (S[C], A[C1H1]); the brackets are load-bearing for ping-pong and multi-substrate reactions, whose residues and atom balance the search reads from them. EnzymeRates.Fitting.FittingProblem — Method
FittingProblem(mechanism, table, Keq::Real; absolute::Bool = false, reference = nothing)

Construct a FittingProblem from an enzyme mechanism, tabular data and the reaction's equilibrium constant Keq, which the fit holds fixed. It compiles the mechanism's rate law once, for every fit of the problem.

The table, any Tables.jl table, must have at least one row and the columns group, Rate, and one per metabolite of metabolites(mechanism), so no metabolite may be named group or Rate.

With absolute = false, the default, the rates are relative: the fit ignores a factor on each group's rates, and reference, optional, sets the scale of the returned rate constants. It is a vector of anchors concentrations => turnover: a NamedTuple of concentrations in the data's units, in which a metabolite left out is zero, and the turnover per unit enzyme measured there. With absolute = true the rates are per-enzyme turnover. Normalized vs absolute rate explains the two modes.

Errors unless

  • every rate is finite and nonzero (the loss takes its log);
  • every concentration is a finite number ≥ 0 (zero is valid);
  • Keq is positive and finite;
  • each reference anchor names metabolites of the mechanism at finite concentrations ≥ 0, with a finite, nonzero turnover;
  • reference is nothing in absolute mode.
fp = FittingProblem(m, data, 2.0; reference = [(A = 10.0, B = 5.0) => 42.0])

Give the concentrations, Keq and any reference concentrations in one unit, M recommended; see Units and the search box in Getting Started.

EnzymeRates.Mechanisms.Mechanism — Type
Mechanism(catalytic::CatalyticScheme, allosteric::Allostery)

An enzyme mechanism: its catalytic scheme (catalytic), a reaction graph, and its allostery (allosteric). It is plain when allosteric.allosteric_states is empty, and allosteric (MWC) otherwise. @enzyme_mechanism builds one from its text, and show prints that text.

allosteric.allosteric_states[l] is the allosteric state of the steps of catalytic whose kinetic group label is l; Mechanisms.Allostery says what a state means for the steps each conformation runs. The constructor stores the mechanism in canonical form, so two writings of one mechanism are == and hash alike: the kinetic groups numbered by their first step, the states moved with them, and the regulatory sites sorted. A plain mechanism and an allosteric one with the same catalytic scheme differ. It throws an ErrorException when:

  • an allosteric mechanism does not give each kinetic group one known state, or has an oligomeric state below 1;
  • a plain one has an oligomeric state other than 1 or a regulatory site;
  • a ligand cannot name a metabolite, names an enzyme form or sits at two sites.

Whether its rate law is defined is check_valid_rate_equation's question, not the constructor's.
