## Question

Can a key recognise rate laws that are the same function up to a renaming of their fitted parameters, exactly and cheaply? Today's `eq_hash` is a hash of the rendered equation text, so it only catches identical text.

**How the key works:**
1. **Normalize.** Multiply by the monomial that brings every variable's lowest exponent to zero, divide by the integer content, and make the denominator positive. An MWC law is normalized per state over (N, d, D), plus each regulatory-site factor on its own; `L` and `Keq` are never renamed.
2. **Label parameters canonically.** Colour refinement runs on the term–parameter incidence structure. Edges are labelled by exponent; terms are labelled by component, coefficient and fixed-name monomial. If ties remain, an individualization search takes the lexicographically smallest leaf.
3. **Hash.** The key is the SHA-256 of the canonical text. The canonical order of each law's fitted names doubles as the name map between members of a class.

I computed four keys:
- **K1:** the fitted-name law (dependents substituted).
- **K2:** the all-names law. Single-symbol ties are folded; Haldane/Wegscheider dependents stay as their own symbols, and each merge is checked against those definitions.
- **K1c / K2c:** the same two after common-factor cancellation.

Production estimate: about 75 lines for the key and about 60 for tests. Embedding K2's relations would add about 12 lines; factor cancellation, if wanted, about 90.

## Results

Populations: 2,717 mechanisms. Every law was checked numerically before keying:
- today's laws against the package's sample rates: worst relative error 7.9e-14;
- redesign plain laws against the oracle: ≤ 6.9e-33;
- redesign MWC laws against P5b's own evaluation: ≤ 1.4e-150.

Pooling init_bibi_pp with expand_bibi (same reaction) gives no merges across the two populations.

**Zero false merges.** That covers 392 merges on today's laws and 417 on the redesign's (K2). Each merge passed three checks:
- the two laws are identical polynomials under the name map;
- for K2, the dependent definitions hold in both directions;
- the rates agree at 3 random points through a route that never uses the key: the BigFloat oracle for plain laws, the package's own law for today's MWC, P5b's evaluator for the redesign's MWC. The worst relative difference was 1.6e-149.

As a negative control, swapping two parameters in the name map was caught every time: 60/60 per MWC set, 2/2 per plain set.

**Zero missed renamings.** A rename-invariant fingerprint (all fitted values equal) found every pair with equal fingerprints but different keys. Matching per-parameter profiles, then checking numerically, showed that none was a renaming.

**Stable and exact:**
- 9,904 of 9,904 keys were identical across two separate Julia processes.
- 43,472 random renamings left the key unchanged, with an exact induced map.

**Search was never needed.** Colour refinement alone separated the parameters in all 21,736 labelings. No law had a parameter symmetry, and no map ever sent a `k` to a `K`.

**Cost per law:**
- median 59–136 µs by population; 99th percentile from 150 µs (allo_bibi) to 3.5 ms; maximum 5.4 ms on the 496-term expand_bibi laws.
- On a 496-term law the 5.0 ms splits as: normalization 0.7, incidence 0.5, refinement 1.8, text plus SHA 2.0.

**What merges:**
- allo_bibi (redesign): 256 of 295 merges differ only in an allosteric tag (233 EqualAI↔OnlyA, 23 involving NonequalAI); the other 39 are regroupings. expand_bibi MWC: all 118 are tag-only.
- **In every tag-only merge, every retagged group (131 and 281) is absent from the inactive state in both mechanisms.** The tag cannot change the rate law, but it changes the names.
- Plain merges are two Theorell–Chance "rotation" twin pairs per bi-bi seed set (init_bibi/69 ~ /144, /79 ~ /130). These are the same single-cycle law with the A-first and B-first orders swapped.

**Fit copying moves coordinates.** On allo_bibi (today's laws), 795 fitted positions moved across 270 merges, so today's copy-by-name breaks.

**Normalization by the constant term or by Π[S] does not work in general.** For plain laws:

| Population | Constant term is one monomial | Constant term absent | Π[S] coefficient is one monomial |
|---|---|---|---|
| init_bibi | 191 / 239 | 0 | 179 / 239 |
| init_bibi_pp | 191 / 264 | 25 (ping-pong) | 200 / 264 |
| expand_bibi | 123 / 240 | 15 | 95 / 240 |

Dividing by a multi-term coefficient gives rational-function coefficient maps, so lowest terms by a monomial shift is the simplest normalization that works for every law.

**Non-monomial common factors.** These are spectator-ligand factors such as `(K_Q + Q)` or `(K_A·K_P + A·K_P + P·K_A)`. They appear in 16/239, 16/264 and 3/240 plain laws, 10/460 expand_bibi MWC laws, and no allo_bibi laws. `Factor.jl` removed all of them, and none were left afterwards. Cancelling them added **no** merges, but it halves the affected laws: 344 → 168 terms on the 16 init_bibi laws.

**Same model, but not a renaming.** I tested every pair in a shared concentration-support bucket with different keys. A pair counts as the same model when each law reproduces the other in both directions, at about 1e-15 including 20 held-out concentrations. That found at least 38 pairs (a lower bound, because the fits can stop in local minima):

| Population | Same-model pairs |
|---|---|
| init_bibi | 8 |
| init_bibi_pp | 8 |
| expand_bibi plain | 8 |
| allo_bibi | 13 |
| allo_uniuni | 1 |

Example: init_bibi/104 has Q as an RE spectator and P as SS; /105 has P as RE and Q as SS. Their denominators have 9 and 8 terms, so the two parameterizations are related by a non-monomial map, not a renaming. No rename-proof key can see these.

## Verdict

**PASS.** The key is exact (0 false merges in 809 merges checked in independent ways, 0 missed renamings) and cheap (median about 70 µs; worst 5 ms). It cuts MWC expansion classes by 17–26%.

There are three caveats, listed under design implications.

## Design implications

1. **Key definition.** Normalize to lowest terms by monomial shift, coprime integer coefficients and a positive denominator. Label parameters by colour refinement and keep the search as a 25-line safety net. Hash the canonical text with SHA-256, never with `objectid` or Dict order. The text format then stops being load-bearing.
2. **Compute the key from the derivation, before any codegen.** Only one representative per class then needs compiling and fitting; renamed duplicates skip compilation entirely.
3. **Fit-copying contract.** `memo` stores fits (raw values, loss, cross-validation results) by canonical position. A member reads its values through its own canonical order and recomputes kcat rescaling under its own names. Bounds must be uniform or mapped by name. The cross-validation pool and beam should dedup by this key.
4. **Caveat 1: which law to key.** K1 depends on the solve choosing dependents consistently across equivalent mechanisms. The redesign's content priority loses both Theorell–Chance twin pairs in each bi-bi seed set (4 merges in 503 seeds). K2 recovers them, but its key must include the relation lattice in canonical order.
5. **Caveat 2: common factors.** Spectator-site factors stop "lowest terms" from being canonical in principle, although they cost no merges here.
6. **Caveat 3: renaming is not model identity.** About 3% of plain laws and about 1% of allo_bibi laws have a twin that is the same model under a non-renaming reparameterization.
7. **Enumeration.** The main duplicate source is tags on groups that the inactive state never contains. Canonicalizing those tags in enumeration, or rendering their names without a tag, would stop these duplicates being generated at all.

## Open questions for Denis

1. **K1 or K2?** K1 keys the IndependentParameters law and is simplest. K2 keys the AllParameters law with folds plus relations: about 12 more lines, copies fits via `all_parameters`, and catches the dependent-choice twins. I lean K1 for v1.
2. **Should the derivation cancel non-monomial common factors?** It costs about 90 lines and halves the terms of 45 plain and 10 MWC laws, but changes no merges. This mainly matters for codegen (P7).
3. **Canonicalize tags on inactive-absent groups at enumeration?** This is an enumeration change.
4. **Same-model non-renaming pairs.** Leave them out of scope (they will show up as equal-loss fits), or add a post-fit check?

## Surprises and failures

- Seed populations barely merge (0.8%). The duplicates are almost all in MWC expansions.
- Today's package merges the Theorell–Chance twins only by luck of its dependent choice; the redesign's principled choice breaks them under K1.
- My first factor cancellation handled only `(x + K)` factors and left 8 of 16 laws unresolved. The general version, which divides by candidates taken from the coefficients, resolves all of them.
- The Levenberg–Marquardt estimate crashed on non-finite Jacobians for MWC laws; I fixed it and reran. It is also stochastic: the today and redesign runs found slightly different pair sets, so I report their union.
- I first overwrote the earlier run's data; the stability check uses the four populations I saved in time.
