## Question
Can one exact log-linear solve over bare per-step constants (cycle rows + kinetic-group equality rows, one fixed content-only priority) give deterministic, correct dependent parameters? And does it fix the known parameter-count phantoms?

How the solve works:
- **Columns:** one per bare constant, named by content, so storage order, direction and grouping never enter a name: `k:<side> -> <side>` for an SS rate constant, `Kd:` for an RE binding, `K:` for another RE step.
- **Rows:** fundamental cycles of a spanning forest, plus group equality rows. Group members are lined up by the metabolites they take up and give off.
- **Solve:** reduced row echelon form over `Rational{Int64}`, every operation checked (an overflow throws `OverflowError`; I tested this). Then I fold single-symbol dependents and label each one `:kinetic_group`, `:wegscheider` or `:haldane`.
- **Priority** (larger key is eliminated first; on a tie the smaller name is kept), key `(kind, occupied, ss, against)`:
  - **kind:** isomerization 3 > other chemistry 2 > plain binding 1;
  - **occupied:** 1 if no form of the step is unbound, so free-enzyme constants stay independent longest;
  - **ss:** SS rate constants go before RE constants;
  - **against:** the rate constant running against the physical forward direction goes first. Forward is judged by the package's tier-1 test (substrates remaining, then products made), extended by free substrates minus free products to orient bindings and releases.

**Production estimate:** about 130–150 lines for the plain solve, fold, labels and AllParameters, reusing the constructor's step orientation; flux-free detection adds about 40 lines on top of a block decomposition (about 70 standalone). Today's equivalent is 207 code lines (the thermo file minus the `:OnlyA` guard and the enumeration counter, plus Pass 2 and the allosteric stacking and filter). So this piece saves little code; its value is determinism and correctness.

## Results
Coverage: all 806 plain mechanisms in every population. For stress testing only, I also solved the catalytic graphs of the 1993 allosteric mechanisms as plain graphs.

- **(a) Counts.**
  - My independent count equals both `length(fitted_params)` and `_independent_param_count` in 806 of 806. My AllParameters count equals the package's Full list in 806 of 806. With no differences, there was nothing for the identifiable rank to arbitrate.
  - **ROADMAP issue 3 (dead-end SS step) reproduces.** On the hand-built `E(I) + J <--> E(J) + I` with `E(J)` a dead end: count 6, BigFloat finite-difference rank 5. Only the ratio of the two constants matters: v with the step at SS equals v with it at RE, at the same ratio, to 1.5e-116.
  - **Bridges alone are not enough.** I generalized them to "flux-free SS steps": contract each RE segment to a node, then flag an SS step that is a self-loop with zero net change, or that sits in a biconnected block where no cycle runs the reaction (Tarjan). Making every kinetic group whose steps are all flux-free into RE gives count = rank in all 5 cases:
    - 3 hand-built, plus handwritten/17 and specs/14 ("redundant SS-bind");
    - plain Tarjan bridges catch only 3 of the 5;
    - 173 more mechanisms have flux-free steps grouped with flux-carrying ones; these are not phantoms (count after conversion = rank in 174 of 175 such mechanisms).
  - **Calibration sample:** 40 of 40 enumerated mechanisms have count = rank. 17 of 53 spec and handwritten mechanisms have structural non-identifiability that neither the solve nor the flux-free rule touches (for example Segel Uni Uni 5 vs 3, RE Random Bi-Bi 10 vs 7). handwritten/17 has a second phantom of this other kind: an RE copy of A binding free E beside the productive RE `E + A` (count 7 vs rank 6 when isolated).
- **(b) Determinism, 0 failures.**
  - 4030 permuted copies (species order, step order, group order, member order, step directions) gave identical independents, exact dependent expressions, folds and labels. The 1993 allosteric graphs passed 5979 permutations.
  - Of 1824 single-group splits, 663 keep the count (all RE binding squares). All 663 give identical independents and expressions; only the labels change, from kinetic group to Wegscheider. 5 merges along folds also give identical results.
  - **The package fails the same test.** On those 663 same-model splits it changes the fitted name set in 312 and the dedup key in 327. In init_bibi/65, for example, the split model folds the free-enzyme `K_EA_to_E_A` into `K_EAQ_to_EQ_A`: same rate function, different name and key.
- **(c) Correctness: all 806 mechanisms, not just 300.**
  - Detailed balance: worst |v(Γ=Keq)|/|v(Keq/10)| = 9.1e-105 over 1612 draws.
  - Package agreement over 3224 samples: my dependents rebuild every one of the package's bare constants to ≤4.1e-154, so both define the same constraint set in every mechanism. The oracle at my constants matches the package's Reduced v to ≤7.9e-14, and the exact package polynomials to ≤1.6e-32. 0 failures.
- **(d) Arithmetic:** the largest numerator or denominator anywhere in the reduction is 2; every exponent is ±1; no overflow. One constant is pinned to 1 (handwritten/24); the package's `keq_tie` fixture pins `K_TC = Keq`, as the package intends.
- **Cycle-space claim holds:** reducing with the Keq column first leaves exactly one Haldane row in 2799 of 2799 graphs, and the gcd of the Haldane multiples is 1 in all 806 plain mechanisms.
- **Dependent choice vs the package:**
  - Same set in 283 mechanisms, different in 523 (the Haldane choice differs in 487, a Wegscheider choice in 93).
  - The package's Haldane dependent is a forward constant (a product-release rate) in 200 of 806, and is `k_EA_to_E_A` (a first-substrate off-rate) in 9 Segel ordered, Theorell–Chance and ping-pong specs.
  - Mine is never a forward constant. It is a chemistry step's reverse constant in 630 of 806 (package 482). All 176 exceptions are cases where the enumerator ties every chemistry constant to a plain binding.
- **Speed:** median 9.9–10.9 µs per solve (max 52) against 74 µs for the package's `_dependent_param_exprs` (warm 70 µs), about 7× faster in the same process.

## Verdict
**PASS.**
- (b) had zero failures.
- (c) agreed everywhere.
- (a) found no count differences to explain; known issue 3 and the redundant SS-bind phantom are fixed by the flux-free extension.

Caveats:
- The joint solve by itself fixes no plain count, because the package's plain counts were already right.
- Allosteric tie rows and limits were not built here, so allosteric known issues 1 and 2 are untested by this probe.

## Design implications
1. With one column per bare constant and equality rows, a tied class behaves like its most primary member. This is exactly what makes same-model regroupings come out identical. The spec should state it, and should name each group by that same class representative rather than by a separate naming rule.
2. `0 = log Keq` cannot occur when group members take up and give off the same metabolites: if every class cancels, every net metabolite change cancels too. Keep it as an assertion only. Pins to 1 or to `Keq^c` are real and must be supported.
3. Plain `Int64` arithmetic is ample. Fold every dependent whose right side is a single symbol, isomerization ties included (the package folds binding ties only). Do not promise one Haldane dependent: 18 mechanisms with parallel chemistry routes have two.
4. Flux-free SS detection belongs in the design, with the general rule above and whole groups only.
5. AllParameters with group rows only reproduces today's Full list, but 27 mechanisms then have an open RE cycle. Adding the RE-only cycle rows closes them (1–3 dependents each).
6. Test pins on dependents and fitted names will move in about 65% of mechanisms; the counts do not move.

## Open questions for Denis
1. In 34 enumerated mechanisms a chemistry step shares constants with an occupied dead-end binding, so its class is ranked like that binding and a name decides the Haldane pick. Keep the single-pass rule (my recommendation), or add a second pass that ranks such classes as chemistry?
2. Flux-free SS groups: make them RE automatically (the rate law is identical; my recommendation), or reject or warn?
3. AllParameters with an open RE cycle: add the RE-cycle rows? My numbers support doing so.

## Surprises and failures
- My first "progress" score summed per-molecule positions. It mis-oriented S1 + S2 → P and eliminated a forward constant; comparing against the package caught it, and I replaced it with the tier-1 comparison.
- My first rank routine scaled each column, which inflated a 1e-87 column to O(1) and gave a false full rank. Fixed.
- Far more same-model splits than I expected (663), and the package's 327 differing dedup keys on them.
- Structural phantoms in 17 of 53 hand-written and spec mechanisms.
- The package-side run covered 782 mechanisms (no handwritten population); it also timed an earlier version of `Thermo.jl`, which made the same solve with a different priority rule. No Julia process is left running.
