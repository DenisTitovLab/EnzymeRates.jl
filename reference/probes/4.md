# P5a: what should `:OnlyA` mean?

## Question

What should `:OnlyA` mean, and does the answer depend on choices it shouldn't? Five sub-questions:
1. Can the true limit be computed exactly, and does it agree with today's deletion + pruning?
2. Is the limit thermodynamically consistent along the whole path?
3. Does the limit depend on which constants are independent?
4. Can a symbolic leading-order (Newton-face) engine reproduce it, and can the same engine compute kcat?
5. What should the guard check?

The answers rest on a validated formulation-1 two-conformation oracle.

### Path definitions

**The task's path definition is not well-defined.** Scaling only the joint solve's independents, with dependents following their rows, depends on which constants are independent. Even guard-admitted cases change:
- `(OnlyA, OnlyA, EqualAI)`: 2.68 vs 1.043, because when `K_I_S` is the Haldane dependent its exponent comes out 0 and S keeps binding I.
- It depends on the order in 27 of 91 admitted hand-built cases, 273/460 expand_bibi and 822/1477 allo_bibi (convention i).
- Even P2's package-like order is not a valid path in 9 of the 58 admitted hand-built mechanisms that have an `:OnlyA` group.

**Order-free definition (the cone).** Impose the convention on every inactive `:OnlyA` constant, hold every other constant (A and I) fixed, and require C·a = 0. Then enumerate the feasible directions:
- a grid plus up to 12 sampled interior points per mechanism;
- up to 16 directions evaluated per mechanism (12 per hand-built case);
- each direction checked against the oracle.

"Not unique" verdicts are certain (two exact directions disagree). "Unique" verdicts are sampled, not proven.

- **Cone feasibility:** the cone is non-empty for every one of the 1937 admitted mechanisms.
- **Size of the departures:** where the limit is not "unique = deletion", the difference from deletion is not small. The median is 100%; 93 of 105 (expand_bibi, i) and 32 of 39 (allo_bibi, i) differ by at least 1e-2.
- **Causes under (i):** 90 of expand_bibi's 105 failures and all 39 of allo_bibi's are mixed kinetic groups: a dead-end plain binding (for example `EQ + A <--> EAQ`) shares its constants with an `:OnlyA` fused or Theorell–Chance step. The tie makes the dead-end affinity follow the chemistry ratio, which the Haldane relation drives to 0 or ∞.
- **Causes under (iu):** the 16 remaining failures are all ping-pong mechanisms whose covalent island connects only through vanishing chemistry (`expand_bibi/306`–`320` and `/700`).
- **No structural shortcut found:** a simple graph rule I tried does not separate the outcomes. It flags 727 + 723 of allo_bibi's 1477 under (iu), all of which equal deletion.

### Normalization and 0/0

- Normalization must precede the limit. Substituting 0/∞ into N_I, Q_I, D_I separately gives 0/0 whenever every inactive spanning tree into free E crosses a vanishing step (the counts are in the "Naive 0/0" column). Under (ii) this is common: 20 of 91 admitted hand-built cases.
- The leading face of the cross-weighted form had no 0/0 and no cancellation in any record.
- The only degenerate outcome is sequestration, ord Q_I < ord D_I. The A branch then drops out of the denominator.

### kcat with the same engine

kcat here means: substrates → ∞ after the regulator and inhibitor corners, products set to 0, maximum over corners.

**Plain mechanisms:** equal to `_kcat_forward` in 258 of 297. My reimplementation of the package's pattern rule matches it in 297/297. The 39 differences:
- 37 are dead-end complexes that capture the enzyme at saturation: the engine gives 0, the package gives the uninhibited turnover.
- 1 has a saturated face with two vertices (`expand_bibi/609`, ratio 0.70).
- 1 is a ping-pong mechanism with an inhibitor copy (`handwritten/15`).
- The 4 package errors ("no kcat components") have kcat = ∞ by the engine: the mechanism never saturates.

**Allosteric mechanisms:** equal in 213 of 218; 5 dead-end cases give 0.

**On limit laws:** with three stages (ε first, then corners, then substrates), the engine matches the oracle at S = 1e12 to 1e-11–1e-13 on 7 cases.

## Verdict

**PARTIAL.** What passes:
- The oracle (V1–V4).
- Exact limits: the symbolic face matches the oracle and stays consistent along every path.
- kcat by the same engine.
- A clear semantics with measured coverage.

What fails:
1. The specified path (scale the independents only) depends on the choice of independents; the cone formulation replaces it.
2. Under (i) as the guard reads tags today (one class per group), 144 of 1937 enumerated mechanisms have no unique limit or sequester.
3. Some guard-admitted ping-pong mechanisms (err1, dead-inactive, the RE-chemistry ping-pong, 16 expand_bibi) have no deletion-equal limit under any convention.
4. Uniqueness is established by sampling, not proven.

## Design implications

1. **Define `:OnlyA` as a limit on a cone**, not by RREF independents. The inactive constants of `:OnlyA` steps go to their limits; every other constant stays fixed; every cycle, group and tie relation holds for every ε.
   - RE binding: K_I → ∞, meaning no affinity in I (MWC exclusive binding).
   - SS binding: kon_I → 0 with koff_I fixed, the same affinity reading. It equals deletion in all 44 admitted grid sets.
   - Chemistry (SS, RE turned SS, fused, Theorell–Chance): both k → 0 with the ratio free. This is a kinetic block; the free ratio absorbs the Haldane shift.
   - Reject "both k → 0" for bindings, convention (ii): its island masses depend on relative rates.
2. **An `:OnlyA` group is untied in the inactive state**, and each member keeps its own class. This takes allo_bibi from 1438 to 1477 deletion-equal limits and expand_bibi from 355 to 444.
3. **The guard checks three things:**
   - (G1) a pure path exists. This is today's Stiemke test, which matches cone feasibility on 108/108 grid mechanisms.
   - (G2) a finite inactive free fraction: ord Q_I = ord D_I.
   - (G3) a unique leading face over the cone.

   When all three pass, the limit equals deletion + pruning in every tested case: 1921 enumerated mechanisms under (iu), plus all grid sets, gates and specs.
4. **One engine computes the inactive limit, the checks and kcat.** It works on the full graph, so the inactive names come from the full graph rather than a pruned one.
5. **Normalize before taking the limit:** take the leading face of the cross-weighted form, never of separately substituted polynomials.

## Open questions for Denis

1. **Should `:OnlyA` be the affinity/kinetic-block limit above, or pure deletion?** Deletion is always thermodynamically consistent and would need no guard. It would also admit the 32 tag sets the guard rejects (the trap among them), and those laws are not limits of the active-state model. I recommend the limit.
2. **Should an `:OnlyA` group be untied in I?** I recommend yes. The alternatives are to reject mixed `:OnlyA` groups, or to keep today's deletion for them, which is not a limit there.
3. **What about the tag sets with no deletion-equal limit** (err1, the dead-inactive ping-pong, two `:OnlyA` chemistry steps around a covalent island)? Options are to reject them (my recommendation, 16 + 3 cases here) or to keep deletion by definition.
4. **What should kcat mean?** The saturating limit can be 0 at dead-end saturation or ∞ when the mechanism never saturates. Today's maximum over common patterns is always finite. `rescale_parameter_values` needs a finite positive kcat, and 48 of 519 mechanisms are affected.

## Surprises and failures

- The specified path (scale only the independents) depends on the choice of independents, so I added the cone formulation.
- Classifying steps one at a time disagreed with the guard on 2 allo_bibi mechanisms; that is how I found the mixed-group problem.
- The integer grid falsely reported an empty cone for 9 all-`:OnlyA` mechanisms whose nullspace has dimension about 14. The exactly-checked sampler fixed this.
- Non-unique counts moved (93 → 79 → 97) as the sampling changed, which is why "unique" stays a sampled claim.
- The limit for err1 is non-uniform as the products go to zero.
- A cheap structural predicate failed.
- Fixed bugs along the way: the order of an empty product (a run of wrong limits), a negative BigInt power, `pkill -f` killing my own shell, and a wait loop matching its own command line.
- Another agent's `Pkg.test` held the lock for about 6 minutes.
