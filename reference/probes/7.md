# P4: normalization and the textbook bridge

## Question
1. Is there one principled rule for normalizing the denominator? Denis's version: a "1" when there is a constant term, and a principled dimensionless form otherwise, such as for ping-pong.
2. Is the stated constant-term criterion (exactly one terminal SCC of the metabolite-free directed edges after Cha contraction) exact?
3. Does the numerator factor as (ΠS − ΠP/Keq)·Q after the joint solve?
4. Can the textbook bridge be done in code by exact polynomial identity?

## Results
The population is all 806 plain mechanisms: specs 31, handwritten 24, init_uniuni 1, init_unibi 7, init_bibi 239, init_bibi_pp 264, expand_bibi 240. Every joint-solved law matches the BigFloat oracle to a relative error of 2.5e-33 or better.

### Part 1: constant-term criterion
757 mechanisms have a constant term and 49 do not.

| Criterion | Agrees | Wrong on |
|---|---|---|
| c1, as stated in the brief | 766/806 | 40, all of them predicting a constant term that is absent |
| c0: Gunawardena on the full graph, RE steps as reversible pairs | 805/806 | handwritten/21 |
| c2: segment exit by metabolite-free RE directions | 805/806 | specs/14 |
| **c3: refined, see below** | **806/806** | none |

**Why c1 fails.** It is right only on the 9 all-SS mechanisms without a constant term, where contraction does nothing. It fails on all 40 RE-bearing ones:
- 31 are residual ping-pongs. A metabolite-free SS release leaves an RE-bound form, so its Cha weight carries [A]/K and the edge is not concentration-free.
- 9 have a segment joined by an exchange step (an RE Theorell–Chance step, or RE residual ping-pong). There α is Laurent, for example K·B/P. The segment has no zero-concentration limit, and the lowest-terms shift removes the constant term: 766 unshifted, 757 shifted.

**Why c2 fails on specs/14.** Inside an RE segment the equilibrium weights decide the zero-concentration limit, not which RE directions are metabolite-free.

**The corrected criterion (c3).** D in lowest terms has a constant term if and only if both of these hold:
- every RE segment has a form whose net metabolite uptake is componentwise minimal (a least-uptake form);
- the graph of segments has exactly one terminal SCC, where its edges are the metabolite-free directions of SS steps that leave a least-uptake form.

For all-SS mechanisms this is exactly Gunawardena's criterion (54/54).

**Terminal SCC counts under c3:**
- 1 terminal SCC: 757 mechanisms. In all 757 that SCC contains the free enzyme, so the "1" is free enzyme at vanishing concentrations.
- 2 terminal SCCs: 39.
- 3 terminal SCCs: 1 (Segel hexa uni ping-pong).
- No least-uptake form: 9.

Wherever there are 2 or more terminal SCCs, the lowest degree of D equals #tSCC − 1 (40/40).

### Part 2: normalization
Seventeen families are printed in `logs/display.log`: RE and SS uni-uni, iso uni-uni, ordered Bi Bi (SS and RE), random Bi Bi (SS, textbook RE, and the RE-on-free-E spec), Theorell–Chance, ping-pong (conformation, residual SS, residual RE), competitive and non-competitive inhibitors, ordered ter-ter, and hexa uni ping-pong. Each is the IndependentParameters law with the package's names. Examples:
- **RE uni-uni:** `[k_EA_to_E_P/K_EA_to_E_A*A - k_EA_to_E_P/(K_EA_to_E_A*Keq)*P] / [1 + A/K_EA_to_E_A]`
- **Competitive inhibitor (denominator):** `1 + P/K_EP_to_E_P + R/K_ERinh_to_E_Rinh + S/K_ES_to_E_S`
- **Ordered Bi Bi, SS (denominator):** `1 + k_E_A_to_EA/k_EA_to_E_A*A + k_EAB_to_EQ_P*k_EA_B_to_EAB/(k_EA_to_E_A*(k_EAB_to_EA_B + k_EAB_to_EQ_P))*B + …`, with 11 terms.
- **Ping-pong, normalized by ΠS (Dalziel form):** `[V1 − V1/Keq*P*Q/(A*B)] / [Kb/B + Ka/A + … + 1 + …]`, where `V1 = k_EA_to_F_P*k_FB_to_E_Q/(k_EA_to_F_P + k_FB_to_E_Q)`.

**The three checks:**
- (a) Division-free: 17/17 families, and 806/806 by construction. Finite with any one metabolite at zero: 806/806. Finite at all-zero: 757/806. The other 49 have D(0) = 0, so they are 0/0 at the origin under any normalization.
- (b) Display, evaluated and oracle agree to a relative error of 6.4e-41 or better (that floor comes from RE steps at Λ = 1e40), and 3.5e-151 or better for SS laws.
- (c) Every display term is dimensionless in D and has units 1/time in N, in 17/17 families. Numerator and denominator are homogeneous in 806/806 in all three solve modes.

### Part 3: Hill form
- **Exact factorization:** (ΠS − ΠP/Keq) divides N in 806/806. The quotient Q is a polynomial in the concentrations with positive coefficients in 806/806, and has half the terms (8034 → 4017).
- **Keq-free Q:** 582/806, including all 17 families.
- **Haldane dependents:** 824 in total (788 mechanisms with one, 18 with two). By the class of the dependent's own step, with the number whose Q is Keq-free:

| Class of the Haldane dependent | Count | Q Keq-free |
|---|---|---|
| SS isomerization, against the forward direction | 226 | 226 |
| SS chemistry, against | 181 | 175 |
| SS binding, against | 294 | 141 |
| RE binding Kd | 65 | 45 |
| RE chemistry K | 57 | 2 |
| RE isomerization K | 1 | 1 |

- **Why Q carries Keq (224 mechanisms).** Two shapes:
  - expand_bibi/321: the chemistry reverse constant is tied by a kinetic group to a dead-end binding's off-rate, so Q ∝ 1/Keq.
  - handwritten/5: the RE chemistry constant K equals Keq·k/k, so Q = Keq·k.
- **Can another pivot fix it?** Moving a single column to the front fixes 134 of the 224 (116 by an SS binding against-constant, 18 by an RE Kd). 90 cannot be fixed by any single pivot: every catalytic-cycle class is grouped with a dead-end step (for example expand_bibi/438). Combinations were not tried, so 90 is an upper bound.

### Part 4: textbook bridge
**Cleland constants as coefficient ratios** (Cleland 1963; Segel p. 564; DynaFit TN-2015-03):
- V1 = n(AB)/c(AB) and V2 = −n(PQ)/c(PQ).
- Ka = c(B)/c(AB) and Kb = c(A)/c(AB); Kp and Kq likewise at PQ.
- Each inhibition constant is Ki_X = c(T)/c(T·X), taking the first context T whose two terms both exist: Kia uses ∅ then P; Kib uses PQ then ∅; Kip uses AB then ∅; Kiq uses ∅ then B.

These reproduce the ordered Bi Bi table exactly, for example Kib = (k1r+k2r)/k2f and Kip = (k3f+k4f)/k3r. Ping-pong gives Kia = k1r/k1f and Kiq = k4f/k4r, and leaves Kib and Kip undefined, as in the textbook.

**Exact symbolic identities with the derived law** (cross-multiplied, in both AllParameters and IndependentParameters):
- Holds for: uni-uni (2 mechanisms × 2 forms), ordered, Theorell–Chance, ping-pong, and the textbook RE random.
- Controls that correctly fail:
  - RE uni-uni: V2 and Kp are undefined.
  - The RE-ordered spec: V2 is undefined.
  - My first, wrong transcription of Cleland's V1V2 uni-uni form.

**Haldane relations that hold identically:**
- ordered: V1·Kp·Kiq/(V2·Kia·Kb) and (V1/V2)²·Kip·Kq/(Ka·Kib);
- Theorell–Chance and RE random: three each;
- ping-pong: (V1/V2)²·Kp·Kq/(Ka·Kb);
- uni-uni: V1·Kp/(V2·Ka).

**The fixtures file's transcriptions:** I parsed `mechanism_definitions_for_test_enzyme_derivation.jl` with `Meta.parseall`, without loading the package.
- All 26 plain `analytical_rate_fn`s are exactly identical to the derived law in both modes.
- Doubling any one constant a transcription uses breaks the identity in 26/26.
- Mapping by the as-written step direction agrees with the test bridge's product-swap rule on every SS step: 0 mismatches.

**Coverage and scale:**
- Only 35 of the 767 bi-bi laws are literally a Cleland form: Theorell–Chance 19, ping-pong 7, ordered 5, RE random 3, plus handwritten/21, which matches both ping-pong and Theorell–Chance.
- D is multilinear (the condition for Cleland constants to mean anything) in 279/806.
- Evaluating a textbook formula with naive exact rational functions was killed for lack of memory (exit 137) on the enumerated laws. Modular evaluation at 3 random points did all 767 × 4 forms.

## Verdict
**PASS, with two corrections the spec must carry.**
- The criterion as stated is wrong in 40 mechanisms. The c3 wording is exact on 806/806.
- One rule works everywhere: rule A.
- The Hill factorization is universal.
- The textbook bridge works by exact identity: 26/26 transcriptions and 5 textbook families. But it applies to few enumerated mechanisms, and its identity check must not use naive rational arithmetic.

## Design implications
1. **Rule A.** Use the constant term as the reference monomial, else ΠS. The display divides by c_ref·x^{m*}. Generated code stays unnormalized, or divides by monomial content only, never by a polynomial c_ref.
   - The reference depends only on the concentration support of D. Monomial substitution does not change that support, so the choice is rename-proof and independent of the dependent choice and of kinetic grouping.
   - The "1" is free enzyme at zero concentration in all 757 cases. Without a constant term it is forward saturation, and the numerator constant is kcat_f.
2. **If the spec states a criterion, it should state c3.** The derivation itself should just test the polynomial. Keep c3 as a property test.
3. **Code generation should use N = (ΠS − ΠP/Keq)·Q**, since Q is always positive. The spec must not promise that Q is free of Keq.
4. **The bridge primitive is the coefficient map.** Kinetic constants are coefficient ratios, using Cleland's context table. Identity checks evaluate exactly modulo a 61-bit prime at random points.
5. **Proposed bridge API:**
   - `rate_law_coefficients(m)` returns the rule-A-normalized `num` and `den` maps, from concentration monomial to an `Expr` in fitted names.
   - `kinetic_constants(m; A, B, P, Q)` returns V1, V2, Ka…Kiq and Keq as `Expr`s in fitted names, with `nothing` for a constant the law does not define. `kinetic_constants(m, params)` returns their values.
   - `is_rate_law(m, f; constants = :cleland)` checks a textbook formula `f(k, concs)` exactly. A `:microscopic` option would need the mechanism to keep the as-written step order and direction.

## Open questions for Denis
1. Rule A or rule B (always ΠS)? I recommend A: it keeps today's "1 + …" strings and needs no fallback in 806/806. B puts V1 in every numerator but needs a fallback in 5 mechanisms.
2. Should the Haldane pivot priority prefer constants that keep Keq out of Q? It would raise Keq-free Q from 582 to at least 716, but the key would come from the bare law rather than the step alone. Exactness and positivity are unaffected either way.
3. How far should the bridge go: Cleland-named forms only (35 of 767 bi-bi laws), generic kcat/Km at ΠS for multilinear laws (279/806), or only `rate_law_coefficients`?
4. Should the Mechanism keep the as-written step order and direction so positional textbook checks work outside the tests?

## Surprises and failures
- The literal criterion fails on every RE-bearing mechanism without a constant term (40 of 40). My first refinement (c2, by direction) failed on specs/14; equilibrium weights are what decide.
- Naive exact rational-function arithmetic was killed for lack of memory on the enumerated laws. The census now uses the field mod 2^61−1; the 5 textbook families stay fully symbolic.
- The identity check caught my own transcription error in Cleland's V1V2 uni-uni form.
- I first overcounted Haldane dependents by counting tied group members that carry Keq. The corrected count of 18 mechanisms with two Haldane dependents matches P2.
- Steady-state display forms needed exact multivariate division and linear-factor cancellation to read like the textbook. Even so, only 569/806 have a clean single-monomial reference.
- No probe Julia process is left running; the lock was never contended.
