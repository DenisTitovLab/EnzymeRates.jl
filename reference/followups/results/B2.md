Denis, enumeration does allow RE chemistry, and it emits this exact group. A kinetic group has a single RE/SS flag covering all its steps (types.jl:741-754), and the enumerator produces your example with both flags:

- **RE**, init_bibi/56: `(E(A) + B ⇌ E(P, Q), E(Q) + B ⇌ E(B, Q))`. The fused binding is RE chemistry. Its constant `K_EPQ_to_EA_B` is fitted, and it is also the dead-end Kd of B at E(Q).
- **SS**, init_bibi/57: `(E(A) + B <--> E(P, Q), E(Q) + B <--> E(B, Q))`. Here the dead-end binding becomes SS along with the fused one.

This is intended. The RE form is the package's own test fixture `_testhelper_fused_and_plain_binding` (test/test_rate_eq_derivation.jl:1131-1135), and docs/src/developer.md:117-120 states the rule.

## How it happens (mechanism_enumeration.jl)

1. **Seeds have no fused bindings.** Every seed step is RE except the first isomerization (:228-233). Dead-end bindings are added as RE (:371). `_seed_groups` groups steps by (consumed, released, flag) (:401-407).
2. **Merging creates the mixed group.** `_seed_variants` folds each isomerization into its product form (:659-666). `E(A) + B ⇌ E(A, B)` becomes `E(A) + B ⇌ E(P, Q)` and stays in B's group next to the dead-end binding.
3. **The group's flag is chosen as a unit.** Every step is set to RE (:868), then minimal sets of whole groups are flipped to SS (:883, :515-526).
4. **What the screen blocks.** It only rejects chemistry that is RE with both substrates and products (condition C, :753-782). So an RE fused binding needs the product releases from its merged complex to be SS. Any RE Theorell–Chance step fails (:781, :844), so every enumerated Theorell–Chance step is SS.
5. **Later moves.** `_expand_re_to_ss` flips whole all-RE groups (:519-520), so a mixed group flips as one. The split move can separate its two members (:1161-1176).

**Not allowed:**
- A Theorell–Chance step grouped with a plain binding (they differ in `_step_kind`, types.jl:707).
- A group mixing an RE fused step with an SS plain step.

I confirmed with the package that the constructor rejects both.

RE isomerizations appear only as the second isomerization of the 7 ping-pong seeds. All 7 are degenerate, so the search fits their flip children instead (identify_rate_equation.jl:670-684). RE fused bindings appear in 76/239 bi_bi and 90/264 bi_bi_pp init mechanisms, and none of those is degenerate.

## P5a check

My own classifier reproduces both of P5a's counts: 90 of 105 failures in expand_bibi and 39 of 39 in allo_bibi have a mixed group. P5a's description (probes/4.md:120) needs three corrections:
- **The chemistry step is always a fused binding** (148 + 39 groups). None of those 129 mechanisms contains a Theorell–Chance step anywhere. "Or Theorell–Chance" comes from P5a's step classifier, whose "kind 2" lumps fused, Theorell–Chance and residual-changing steps together (Thermo.jl:26-27). It does not describe the data.
- **The plain member is not always a dead end.** 12 of the 90 expand_bibi failures have only catalytic plain members, as in init_bibi/170.
- **The flags differ by population.** expand_bibi's failing mixed groups are 106 SS and 42 RE; all of allo_bibi's are SS.

A mixed `:OnlyA` group does not always fail: 24 of 114 (expand_bibi) and 32 of 71 (allo_bibi) give a unique limit equal to deletion. Under P5a's "untied" convention (iu), 128 of the 129 failures are fixed. The one left is a ping-pong island case, expand_bibi/700.

## What this means for the decision

Mixed groups are a normal product of merging, not a corner case. Half of bi-bi init mechanisms have one, with either flag, and the RE ones are fitted directly. They exist because `_merge_isomerization` keeps the seed's kinetic groups, and that changes what the shared constant means:
- In the seed, the group says B binds E(A) and E(Q) with the same Kd.
- After merging, it says the dead-end Kd of B at E(Q) equals the combined binding-plus-chemistry constant [E(A)][B]/[E(P, Q)].

That reading is my inference; I have not tested it against data. Under `:OnlyA`, the plain member's inactive affinity then follows a chemistry ratio that goes to zero, which is P5a's failure mode. The Haldane guard also drops the whole group from its check (thermodynamic_constr_for_rate_eq_derivation.jl:443-449, 478-479).

You have three options:
- **Untie the group in the inactive state.** This fixes 128 of 129, but the skeptic's AD1 and AD11 cases then have no valid limit.
- **Give the fused binding its own group when merging.** This removes mixed groups at the source. I have not measured how it changes the parameter counts or the seed set.
- **Forbid mixed `:OnlyA` groups.**
