For the 757 laws with a constant term, the rule you chose already gives dimensionless terms: d_0 is a combination of kinetic constants, and every term of D/d_0 is dimensionless. For the 49 without one, you can divide by a "virtual constant term", N = c(A)·c(B)/c(AB). That gives dimensionless, division-free terms. A leading 1 is impossible for these 49 laws.

## 1. Laws with a constant term (757 of 806)

**Units check:** in 757/757, every term of D/d_0 has units conc⁰·time⁰ and every numerator term has units 1/time (`census_c4.jl:31-33`). This follows because all 806 laws are homogeneous, so each term reads c^m/K_m with K_m = d_0/d_m.

- **SS uni-uni** (`E + S <--> E(S)`, `E(S) <--> E + P`), d_0 = k_ES_to_E_S + k_ES_to_E_P:
  - D/d_0 = `1 + S/K_S + P/K_P`
  - K_S = (k_ES_to_E_S + k_ES_to_E_P)/k_E_S_to_ES, the Briggs–Haldane Km.
  - K_P = the same sum divided by the Haldane dependent k_E_P_to_ES.
  - The numerator prints as k_ES_to_E_P·S/K_S − …, i.e. kcat times a dimensionless group.
- **SS ordered bi-bi** (`E + A <--> E(A)`, `E(A) + B <--> E(A, B)`, `E(A, B) <--> E(Q) + P`, `E(Q) <--> E + Q`), d_0 = k_EA_to_E_A·k_EQ_to_E_Q·(k_EAB_to_EA_B + k_EAB_to_EQ_P):
  - The 11 terms are exactly Cleland's: `1 + A/K_ia + B·K_a/(K_ia·K_b) + Q/K_iq + A·B/(K_ia·K_b) + …`
  - K_ia = k_EA_to_E_A/k_E_A_to_EA and K_iq = k_EQ_to_E_Q/k_E_Q_to_EQ.
  - K_ia·K_b = k_EA_to_E_A·k_EQ_to_E_Q·(k_EAB_to_EA_B + k_EAB_to_EQ_P)/(k_E_A_to_EA·k_EA_B_to_EAB·(k_EAB_to_EQ_P + k_EQ_to_E_Q)).
- **RE random bi-bi** (init_bibi/55, with each binding's K shared by a kinetic group), d_0 = 1:
  - `1 + A/K_EA_to_E_A + B/K_EB_to_E_B + P/K_EP_to_E_P + Q/K_EQ_to_E_Q + A·B/(K_EA_to_E_A·K_EB_to_E_B) + P·Q/(K_EP_to_E_P·K_EQ_to_E_Q)`

## 3. Shape of the reference coefficient d_0

- d_0 is a single monomial in 539/757 and a sum in 218: 190 with 2 terms, 14 with 3, 14 with 4–7.
- P4's 569 counts all 806 laws: these 539 plus the 30 laws without a constant term whose c(ΠS) is a monomial.
- When d_0 is a sum, each group is still X/K_X with K_X = d_0/d_X. The 218 laws have 751 such linear groups:
  - 179 reduce to a ratio of monomials, a K_d such as K_ia.
  - 30 have the Km shape, a sum of rate constants over one rate constant.
  - About 540 remain ratios of sums. P4's reducer cancels only monomials and linear factors, so a few of these may simplify further.

## 2. Laws without a constant term (49)

**Why you cannot get both a leading 1 and division-free terms.** Every term of D contains at least one concentration.
- If you divide by constants only, every term still holds a concentration, so none of them is 1.
- If you divide by something containing a concentration monomial, some term gets a negative exponent. That is because every metabolite is missing from some term (806/806 laws stay finite with any one metabolite at zero). The result is Dalziel's 1/A, which is NaN at A = 0.
- The virtual constant term also depends on which side you build it from. In specs/7, the product side gives c(P)c(Q)/c(PQ) = (V2/V1)·c(A)c(B)/c(AB).

**K-form of the classic ping-pong** (specs/7: `E + A <--> E(A)`, `E(A) <--> F + P`, `F + B <--> F(B)`, `F(B) <--> E + Q`), dividing by N = c(A)c(B)/c(AB). This identity is exact at 3 random points (`pingpong_form.log`):

`v/E_t = V1·(a·b − p·q) / (a + b + a·b + p + q + (K_a/K_dA)·a·p + (K_b/K_dB)·b·q + (V1/V2)·p·q)`

- a = A/K_a, b = B/K_b, p = P/K_p, q = Q/K_q.
- K_a = c(B)/c(AB) = k_FB_to_E_Q·(k_EA_to_E_A + k_EA_to_F_P)/(k_E_A_to_EA·(k_EA_to_F_P + k_FB_to_E_Q)).
- K_b = c(A)/c(AB) = k_EA_to_F_P·(k_FB_to_E_Q + k_FB_to_F_B)/(k_F_B_to_FB·(k_EA_to_F_P + k_FB_to_E_Q)).
- K_p = N/c(P) and K_q = N/c(Q), with K_p·K_q = Keq·K_a·K_b.
- V1 = k_EA_to_F_P·k_FB_to_E_Q/(k_EA_to_F_P + k_FB_to_E_Q).
- K_dA = k_EA_to_E_A/k_E_A_to_EA and K_dB = k_FB_to_F_B/k_F_B_to_FB.

**Census of the 49** (`kform_lib.jl:70-97`). A γ's value does not depend on how N and the K_X are chosen.

| Outcome (full law, products included) | Laws | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Every γ exactly 1 | 3 | init_bibi_pp/61: 7 terms, both relations exactly 1, D/N = `b + q + a·b + b·q + p·q + a·b·p + a·p·q`. init_bibi_pp/62 and handwritten/23: 5 terms for 5 unknowns, so trivially. |
| Some γ a pure number other than 1 | 0 | — |
| γ's are ratios of constants | 45 | specs/7 above. handwritten/11 (`E(A) ⇌ F + P`, with F = E(; residual = A − P)): γ(A·P) = k_FB_to_E_Q/k_EA_to_E_A and γ(B·Q) = (k_FB_to_E_Q + k_FB_to_F_B)/k_FB_to_F_B, which are again K_a/K_dA and K_b/K_dB. |
| No N exists at all | 1 | handwritten/21 (`E(A) + B ⇌ E(Q) + P`): every term holds exactly one of B or P, so v depends only on B/P. |

With the products set to zero (the initial-rate law):
- γ = 1 in 21: 15 bi-bi laws read exactly a + b + a·b, and the hexa-uni ping-pong reads a·b + a·c + b·c + a·b·c.
- γ's are ratios in 19, from substrate-inhibition terms.
- N is undefined in 9, where the rate does not depend on B.

For comparison, even laws with a constant term are pure K-forms in only 139 of 757; the other 618 carry ratios. Examples are γ(A·B) = K_ia/K_a in ordered bi-bi, and 1/α (K_EB_to_E_B/K_EAB_to_EA_B) in the textbook RE random bi-bi. These γ's are parameters of the mechanism, not leftovers of the normalization. My test agrees with P4's census3 on the 313 laws it covered: 35 pure.

## 4. Recommendation

Divide both numerator and denominator by N:
- N = d_0 when the law has a constant term (757 laws).
- Otherwise N = Π_{X∈S} c(ΠS/X) / c(ΠS)^(n−1), which equals c(ΠS)·ΠK_X with Cleland's K_X = c(ΠS/X)/c(ΠS). This covers 40 of the 49, and N has a constant term's units in 40/40.

Every printed term is then dimensionless and free of division by concentrations:
- the ΠS term prints as Π(X/K_X);
- each ΠS/X term prints as the product over the other substrates;
- ping-pong prints as the formula above, with no leading 1.

The 9 laws with a rapid-equilibrium exchange step (P4's "no least-uptake form" set) give N = 0 under this rule, because K_B = 0. For those you can:
- keep dividing by the coefficient only, or
- use a general construction: the coefficient combination with a constant term's units, read off the first affinely independent monomials, substrates first. It covers 8 of the 9 and equals Cleland's N in 37 of the 40. The other 3 are Segel's ter ping-pongs. It costs about 40 lines of exact linear algebra.

There is a printer limitation, seen in the strings rather than measured. P4's `reduce_ratio` leaves factors such as (k_FB_to_E_Q + k_FB_to_F_B) uncancelled in the printed 1/K_a, so clean strings need a real polynomial GCD. This affects only the display; the generated code never divides by a polynomial.
