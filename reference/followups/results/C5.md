## 1. Two separate things

- **The coefficient map** is production code. It takes a derived law, reads off its coefficients and names the Cleland ratios (V1, Ka, Kia, …).
- **The test bridge** is test code. It tells a Segel transcription which package constant its `k1f` is.

The bridge never uses coefficients, so dropping Cleland formulations leaves every test written in microscopic constants alone.

**Today's bridge for Segel Ordered Bi Bi** (`mechanism_definitions_for_test_enzyme_derivation.jl:307-340`):

## 2. Mapping by role

The macro block already holds the order and the direction as text. The test reads the block's `steps:` lines, so `E(A) + B` becomes `(:E, [(:A,false)], [(:B,false)])`. For written step i it then finds the stored step with the same two sides and reads that step's constants in the written direction. This is the plain-mechanism core of the helper I ran:

```julia
function _testhelper_written_constants(m, written, nt)
    mech = _testhelper_lift(m); groups = ER.steps(mech); fes = ER._free_enz_set(mech)
    from(s) = _testhelper_side(ER.from_species(s), ER.consumed(s))
    to(s) = _testhelper_side(ER.to_species(s), ER.released(s))
    out = Pair{Symbol, Symbol}[]
    for (i, (lhs, rhs)) in enumerate(written)
        g, s = only((g, s) for (g, grp) in enumerate(groups) for s in grp
                    if Set([from(s), to(s)]) == Set([lhs, rhs]))
        rep = ER._group_rep(groups[g], fes)
        if ER.is_equilibrium(rep)
            push!(out, Symbol("K", i) => ER.name(ER.Krapid(rep, :None), mech))
        else
            f, r = ER.name(ER.Kfor(rep, :None), mech), ER.name(ER.Krev(rep, :None), mech)
            from(s) == lhs || ((f, r) = (r, f))
            push!(out, Symbol("k", i, "f") => f, Symbol("k", i, "r") => r)
        end
    end
    (; (k => nt[v] for (k, v) in out if haskey(nt, v))...)
end
```

The written direction replaces the swap guess, and nothing needs source order from the package. The parser can then return only the mechanism. Its one other consumer is `_testhelper_allo_from_source` (5 call sites, `test_rate_eq_derivation.jl:1998-2136`), which attaches tags by position; I expect those can write the tags inline (`E(S) <--> E(P) :: OnlyA`), but I did not run that.

## 3. Identifiability

v depends on the constants only through the normalized coefficients, so the identifiable rank equals the rank of the coefficient-ratio map. I checked this: they are equal in 806 of 806 plain mechanisms (`coef_rank.jl`). The last column is how many Cleland constants stay free once the textbook Haldanes are imposed, with Keq fixed (`cleland_dims.jl`).

| mechanism | fitted | rank | ratios (N+D−1) | free Cleland constants |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Segel Uni Uni | 5 | 3 | 4 | 3 |
| Segel Ordered Bi Bi | 7 | 7 | 12 | **8** |
| Segel Ping Pong | 7 | 6 | 9 | 7 |
| RE Random Bi-Bi (specs/20) | 10 | 7 | 11 | no Cleland form |
| Random-order Bi-Bi | 12 | 11 | 29 | no Cleland form |

- **Cleland constants are not even a minimal set for their own families.** Ordered bi-bi has 10 named constants. Beyond the two textbook Haldanes they obey a third, non-monomial relation that I derived and checked to 1e-156: Kb·Kia·V1/(Kib·Ka) = V1 + V2 − V1·Kq/Kiq − V2·Ka/Kia.
- **They fix none of the 5 rank-deficient enumerated plain laws.**
  - `init_bibi_pp/61-62` have no Cleland form.
  - `expand_bibi/303-305` have the ping-pong form, 7 fitted against rank 6, and the Cleland count is also 7.
  - The skeptic traced all 5 to one shape: an RE isomerization `E(B; residual = A - P) ⇌ E(Q)` that the data cannot separate from its neighbour. A structural rule could fold it.
- **They overcount enumerated laws that are rank-full today:**
  - `init_bibi/55` (RE random): 5 → 7
  - `init_bibi_pp/261` (ping-pong): 5 → 7
  - `expand_bibi/349` (ordered): 6 → 8
  - the Theorell–Chance laws I checked (8 of 19): 5–6 → 7
- **The allosteric deficiencies have no Cleland form:** 243 of 1,993, of which 211 are the one-subunit (n = 1) L phantom.

**What fitting in coefficient-ratio space would buy:** the fitted count would equal the rank everywhere. Today a law of rank 6 fitted with 7 constants is filed under 7 by the per-count frontier (`identify_rate_equation.jl:588-602`) and by the 1-SE rule (`:847-854`), which over-penalizes it.

**What it costs:**
- A basis must be chosen per law: 7 of 12 ratios for ordered, 11 of 29 for random-order. The other ratios follow from polynomial relations.
- Some ratio vectors match no positive rate constants. For ordered bi-bi, V1 < V2·Kiq/Kq (= k4f) and V2 < V1·Kia/Ka (= k1r) must hold; none of 100,000 random positive draws broke them, so points past them have no mechanism behind them.
- Bounds and starting points change. Today any positive vector of rate constants is a valid law.

**Verdict:** Cleland formulations give no clear identifiability advantage, so dropping them costs nothing. A general identifiable reparameterization should be a separate, later idea. Its cheapest first step is a rank check (about 40 lines, the skeptic's estimate) whose result model selection uses as the count, while the fitter keeps microscopic constants.
