Normalized vs absolute rate

Enzyme kinetic data is often reported on an arbitrary rate scale. The enzyme concentration in the assay may be unknown, and figures from different preparations may reflect different states of purity, so the absolute value of a measured rate carries no reliable meaning. What survives is the shape of the response: changing the amount of enzyme scales every rate by the same factor and leaves that shape intact.

EnzymeRates.jl handles both cases through two FittingProblem keywords, absolute and reference. With the default absolute = false the data is relative: the fit ignores an arbitrary factor on each group's rates, and reference, one or more turnovers measured at stated concentrations, sets the scale of the returned rate constants. Without a reference the scale is set so that the largest rate the mechanism predicts over the data rows, at E_total = 1, is 1. With absolute = true the data is per-enzyme turnover: the fit scores absolute magnitudes, returns the constants as fitted and takes no reference. IdentifyRateEquationProblem takes the same keywords.

The two modes

Relative mode (the default)

  • Loss: each group's log-ratios are mean-centered before squaring, which removes the arbitrary per-group E_total scale. The loss is invariant to rescaling all rates in a group by the same factor.
  • Output scale: the loss cannot see a common factor on every steady-state rate constant, so the fitter fixes it after the fit: the returned constants make the mechanism reproduce the reference turnovers or, without a reference, put the fastest measured condition at a rate of 1 (below). The factor multiplies only the rate constants — the lowercase-k parameters every steady-state step contributes, in both directions (k_<from>_to_<to>) — and leaves the binding constants K, Keq, E_total, the allosteric L, and regulatory K values unchanged.

The reference

reference is optional in relative mode: a vector of anchors concentrations => turnover.

  • Each anchor's concentrations are a NamedTuple in the units of your data, and a metabolite you leave out is zero.
  • Its turnover is per unit E_total, the active-site concentration: a kcat, or a specific activity in turnover units.
  • When you know no turnover in absolute units, pass a row of your own data, its concentrations => its Rate. The constants then predict rates on that row's scale.
  • Turnover is per unit E_total, so divide a kcat quoted per oligomer by its number of active sites first.
fp = FittingProblem(m, data; Keq = 2.0,
                    reference = [(S = 10.0, P = 0.0) => 42.0])

The fit multiplies every steady-state rate constant by the geometric mean of measured over predicted turnover at the anchors; several anchors combine in log space. A fit whose predicted turnover at an anchor is zero, non-finite or of the other sign has no such factor, for example a law that needs a substrate the anchor leaves at zero. Such a fit fails: fit_rate_equation returns its constants unscaled, with loss = Inf and retcode = :ReferenceFailed, and identify drops the mechanism from the search. Name every substrate and every essential activator in an anchor so each candidate law runs there: a law that needs an activator predicts zero turnover where the anchor leaves it at zero, and its fit fails. In identify, a mechanism that leaves out an optional regulator ignores that regulator's concentration in the anchors.

Without a reference the fit multiplies every steady-state rate constant by one factor so that the largest absolute rate the mechanism predicts over your data's concentration rows, at E_total = 1, is 1. The default-scaled rate constants are therefore in units of the fastest measured condition, not kcats: to read them as turnovers, pass a reference with measured turnovers. A fit whose largest predicted rate is zero or non-finite fails like a failed anchor: constants unscaled, loss = Inf, retcode = :ReferenceFailed.

Relative mode is the right choice when your data reports relative rates (arbitrary units, percent activity, or values normalized per some E_total estimate that you do not trust).

Absolute mode

Pass absolute = true. In absolute mode:

  • Loss: log-ratios are squared without centering. The absolute magnitude of the rate is scored, so the data must report per-enzyme turnover in consistent units.
  • Output: the raw fitted parameters, never rescaled. The data fixes the absolute scale.

Absolute mode is the right choice when your data is already on a per-enzyme turnover basis.

The fitter has no `E_total` input

Every prediction is evaluated at E_total = 1, so the loss compares your measured Rate against the per-enzyme turnover — in effect it fits Rate / E_total. The data table carries no E_total column, and the fitter ignores one if present. Relative mode makes this moot: per-group centering cancels any constant factor, E_total included. Absolute mode does not, so the measured Rate must already be per-enzyme — divide rates recorded at a known enzyme amount by that amount before fitting.

Scoring a fit on other data

EnzymeRates.test_rate_equation(m, params, table; Keq, absolute = false) returns the loss fit_rate_equation minimizes, for the constants params on table: the score of a fit on data it did not see, as cross-validation does for each held-out group. params must hold every name of parameters(m, FittedParameters()), as a fit's params does; other names are ignored. In relative mode the loss centers each group, so it does not depend on the scale of params.

using EnzymeRates

uni_uni = @enzyme_mechanism begin
    substrates: S
    products:   P
    steps: begin
        E + S <--> E(S)
        E(S) <--> E + P
    end
end

params = (k_ES_to_E_P = 3.0, k_ES_to_E_S = 1.0, k_E_S_to_ES = 4.0)
heldout = (group = ["G1", "G1", "G2"], Rate = [0.5, 0.9, 1.1],
           S = [1.0, 5.0, 3.0], P = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0])

EnzymeRates.test_rate_equation(uni_uni, params, heldout; Keq = 2.0)
0.000987166975459473
