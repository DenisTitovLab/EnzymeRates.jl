Developer / Architecture

This page is for maintainers and contributors. It documents the internal architecture of EnzymeRates.jl; the public API lives on the API Reference page.

The package keeps two parallel mechanism representations, split along a deliberate performance boundary. Singleton types drive the compile-time rate-equation derivation; concrete types drive the mechanism enumeration. The two halves and the optimizer layer are described below.

Derivation architecture

A mechanism's rate equation is derived once, at compile time, by the King–Altman/Cha method in @generated functions (src/rate_eq_derivation.jl). To make that possible, a mechanism is encoded as a singleton type — the exported EnzymeMechanism{Sig} or AllostericEnzymeMechanism{Sig} (src/types.jl) — whose one type parameter, Sig, encodes the mechanism's canonical reaction graph as a tuple of integers (_sig_of), so the order or direction in which the steps were written does not change the resulting type. The compiler specializes the derivation per type, so the symbolic King–Altman work runs during precompilation rather than at every call.

This is the most important architectural decision in the package, and it is deliberate. Moving the derivation to compile time leaves rate_equation(m, conc, params) as one arithmetic expression, written in Horner form (_poly_to_horner), that must be allocation-free and fast: under 120 ns per call for every fixture mechanism, and under max(120 ns, 0.8 ns × denominator terms) for the three largest enumerated bi-bi laws (396 denominator terms each) and three ter-bi laws (589, 799 and 858 terms). _testhelper_test_rate_equation_performance (test/test_rate_eq_derivation.jl) measures each law's allocations and time per call, and the tests assert allocs == 0 and the law's bound. The printed equation of rate_equation_string keeps flat sums. That speed is the binding constraint on the whole package: the fitter is a multi-start, global, gradient-free optimizer that evaluates rate_equation millions of times per fit, and a single rate equation can take minutes to fit, so any per-call allocation or microsecond-scale overhead would make fitting — and therefore identify_rate_equation, which fits thousands of candidates — impractical. loss! is held to the same standard: FittingProblem pre-allocates its log_ratios_buffer once and loss! reuses it, so the inner optimization loop allocates nothing. The cost is the flip side of the benefit: each unique singleton type triggers a full symbolic derivation, and a mechanism with many enzyme forms or steps can be slow to compile, exhaust memory, or StackOverflow.

compile_mechanism (internal) is the boundary between the two representations: compile_mechanism(m::Mechanism) = EnzymeMechanism(m) and compile_mechanism(am::AllostericMechanism) = AllostericEnzymeMechanism(am). The lifts back, Mechanism(em) and AllostericMechanism(aem), decode the Sig (_mechanism_from_sig, _allosteric_mechanism_from_sig) into the mechanism it encodes, so a mechanism survives the round trip unchanged. Decoding trusts the Sig, which only _sig_of writes: a plain Sig's decoding checks nothing, and an allosteric one's runs only the composition constructor's tag and site checks. A mechanism of either representation prints as the macro block that builds it (show, src/dsl.jl), from the concrete mechanism, so the compiled types' show takes its argument @nospecialize and @nospecializeinfer and compiles once. _parse_mechanism reads that text back through the macros' walker and checks, never evaluating it, and identify's CSVs store it with a short id (_mechanism_id).

Only the @generated methods (rate_equation, parameters, fitted_params, metabolites) do work per singleton type: rate_equation runs the derivation, fitted_params and parameters run its constraint solve, and metabolites reads names off the lifted mechanism. The lifts read the Sig at run time, and the derivation helpers that take a singleton or its type take it @nospecialize, so they compile once for all mechanisms. Each mechanism type then pays for its generated bodies and the derivation they run, and for the methods that specialize on it: all_parameters, Base's text/plain display, LogConcentrationTurnover, and FittingProblem and loss! below. FittingProblem and loss! specialize on the type on purpose: loss! then reads fitted_params and metabolites as constants and calls the generated rate_equation without dispatch. The optimizer never sees that type. fit_rate_equation takes its FittingProblem @nospecialize and hands Optimization.solve a FitObjective, which holds x -> loss!(x, fp) behind a FunctionWrapper, so every mechanism gives the solver one problem type: solve and the solver compile once per optimizer, not once per mechanism. The peak search that scales a relative fit (_peak_turnover) wraps the mechanism's turnover the same way, so its search loop compiles once.

The joint thermodynamic solve

_dependent_param_exprs (src/thermo.jl) builds one integer system per mechanism (_joint_system): a column per constant of every edge (_Column), with an inactive copy for each edge of a :NonequalAI or :OnlyA group; a row per fundamental cycle and conformation, and a row tying each further member of a kinetic class to its first. It orders the columns by _priority_key (_ordered_columns) and reduces the system exactly (_reduce_joint_system: Rational{Int}, redone in Rational{BigInt} on overflow). Pivot columns are dependents. Columns outside the inactive conformation's law (an :OnlyA edge's inactive copy, an edge on a form the inactive conformation cannot reach) come first, so no kept dependent reads them. Each class is named after its member that comes last in that order (_kept_members), through the name(p, m) chokepoint. The engine derives each conformation's polynomials from the mechanism, a mask of the edges its law keeps (_state_mask; the inactive conformation's is _inactive_mask(am)) and the names of their constants (_edge_names). Every edge keeps its stored direction, so both conformations share the mechanism's orientation and names. The canonical form decides the order and the direction it reads: free E is form 1 and the other forms follow in content order (conformation label, bound metabolites, residual); an edge that neither chemistry nor graph context orients runs from the earlier form; and a tied group's lead turns the group's other tied edges to match it (_align_tied_groups!). The order's last tie-break reads each edge's content as stored: its forms' places in content order, then the metabolites it takes up and gives off (_edge_base_rank). The canonical form thus decides the dependents and so the fitted names: a change to it changes fitted names.

Enumeration engine architecture

Mechanism enumeration uses the concrete types Mechanism and AllostericMechanism (src/types.jl) — internal, not exported. A Mechanism is a reaction graph in canonical form: its sorted substrates and products; table, each metabolite entry its forms and steps hold, once, sorted by name and then by whether it binds as a modifier (Met), a reactant and its modifier copy being two entries; conformations, the sorted conformation labels; forms (Form: a label's index, and the entries it binds, adds and subtracts, each a packed count of table entries, Counts); edges (Edge: two form indices, the entries the step takes up and gives off, and its rapid-equilibrium flag); and group[k], edge k's kinetic group. It holds no reaction, and == and hash read its fields alone, alike in every process. An AllostericMechanism holds a catalytic Mechanism, one allosteric tag per kinetic group, the catalytic multiplicity and the regulatory sites.

The macros, the parser and the moves that change flags, groups or steps write a mechanism as raw input (RawMechanism of RawSteps between RawForms, and RawAllostericMechanism): its steps in written order and direction. Mechanism(raw) and AllostericMechanism(raw) build its canonical graph (_canonicalize) and check it. The moves that tag a mechanism or change its regulatory sites compose the parent's catalytic graph with other tags or sites through the composition constructor, AllostericMechanism(m, tags, multiplicity, sites) (directly or by _with), which runs only its tag and site checks. Going from → to, an edge takes up the entries in consumed and gives off those in released. Steps are classified by these counts. A binding takes up exactly one metabolite and gives off none (_is_binding). It is plain when its target form is its source form with that metabolite added, the residual unchanged and the conformation free to change (_is_plain_binding), and fused otherwise, as when the last substrate binds straight into the product-bound form (E(A) + B → E(P, Q)). The constructor rejects a binding whose forms match a plain binding by name but not by role, such as E + P::Mod ⇌ E(P). An isomerization takes up and gives off nothing (_is_iso); a Theorell–Chance step takes up one metabolite and gives off another. _is_chemistry is true for every edge but a plain binding; the allosteric moves (_chemistry_groups), the :OnlyA test (_onlya_offenders), the joint solve's priority (_priority_key) and _merged_complexes read it to tell catalysis from binding. The derivation reads every binding alike, so a fused binding's rapid-equilibrium constant is a dissociation constant and its steady-state pair a binding and a release rate constant. Every step's constants are named by the two sides of its kinetic class's kept member — each side's enzyme form followed by its free metabolites (K_ES_to_E_S for E + S ⇌ E(S), K_EPQ_to_EA_B for E(A) + B ⇌ E(P, Q), k_EA_B_to_EQ_P for E(A) + B <--> E(Q) + P).

Like the singleton types, the graph is canonical, so the order or direction in which steps are written does not change the resulting mechanism. _canonicalize stores free E first and the other forms in content order, and stores every edge in its physical-forward direction (_orientation): a step with one metabolite takes it up, so a release is stored as the binding it reverses; otherwise the side holding more substrates, then fewer products, comes first, then the form where only products enter or leave, and failing those the earlier form. It sorts the edges and numbers the kinetic groups by their first edge. The edges of one group that metabolite progression leaves tied, such as a conformational change and its mirror at inhibitor-bound forms, turn as one (_align_tied_groups!): each takes up and gives off the same metabolites, and each that changes conformation runs between the same two conformations in the same order, so the group's shared constants describe one physical direction. A tied edge that keeps its conformation, such as one metabolite exchange at two conformations (E(I) + J <--> E(J) + I and F(I) + J <--> F(J) + I), turns by its metabolites alone. A group whose tied edges share no such direction is rejected. The RegulatorySite constructor sorts a site's ligands by name, and the AllostericMechanism constructor sorts the sites.

The constructors check representation only (_check_declarations, _check_representation, and for an allosteric mechanism _check_tags and _check_ligands): names a metabolite can take and conformation labels shaped as labels, none a metabolite's name; every name the steps hold declared, and every declared name taken up, given off or bound by some step; residuals that add substrates and subtract products; the free form E (nothing bound, no residual), and edges that join every form to it. They also enforce the kinetic-group rules. A group's edges take up and give off the same entries and carry one RE/SS flag (_check_uniform_groups), so a fused and a plain binding of one metabolite may share a group and a Theorell–Chance step cannot share one with a binding. A reaction — the pair of an edge's two sides — appears in one edge (_check_each_reaction_once). No two distinct forms render one name (_check_distinct_form_names): a form's name joins its conformation and bound metabolites without a separator, so E with a metabolite star bound and the free conformation Estar would both be Estar and share their constants' names. Names that spell one string two ways, such as NAD, P and NADP, never reach the constructor: @enzyme_reaction and the mechanism macros reject them (_check_uniquely_decodable). Whether a mechanism's rate law is defined is the boundary check's question (_check_well_posed, src/admission.jl): no rapid-equilibrium segment of the catalytic graph lacks a bottom form; and, for an allosteric mechanism, no segment of the inactive conformation's graph lacks one, all free forms lie in one rapid-equilibrium segment outside :OnlyA groups, and no :OnlyA binding leaves a thermodynamic cycle unsatisfiable. The macros, the parser, the enumerator's exits and every derivation entry run it; the constructors and the Sig decoder do not.

These are ordinary value types to avoid excessive precompilation costs. The enumeration builds, expands, and deduplicates many thousands of candidate mechanisms (see The enumeration engine). If each candidate were a singleton parametric type, merely constructing it would trigger compiler specialization — the same precompilation cost the derivation pays — multiplied across thousands of mechanisms, which would be prohibitive. Carrying mechanism structure as runtime values instead means enumeration and deduplication cost no compilation at all. Only the candidates the search actually fits are lifted to singleton types, one at a time, through compile_mechanism.

The moves never emit a child that is provably a reparameterization of its parent. The split move divides a group by binding context and accepts a set of splits only if the independent-parameter count rises; the count comes from the thermodynamic constraint solve, and for a Mechanism it is evaluated without building the child, from a cycle basis computed once per parent (_partition_independent_count). For an AllostericMechanism it is the joint solve's count on the built child, which derives no rate polynomial (_split_gain_test). A candidate whose :OnlyA binding leaves a thermodynamic cycle unsatisfiable, which the boundary check rejects, is never built: the move first runs that check's :OnlyA test (_onlya_offenders) on the parent's graph under the candidate's groups and tags, and the candidate counts as failed. The search extends a failed set by the splits of the groups that share its rapid-equilibrium segments, and a rejected one also by the splits of every :OnlyA group, since the split that balances its cycle may be steady state. The RE→SS move flips whole groups and accepts a set only if the rapid-equilibrium segment count rises and every flipped group carries net flux in the child. In a Mechanism it never offers as a unit a flank of a qualifying chain whose isomerization is steady state (_chain_flank_groups): by the chain lemma that flip adds parameters and no rate law. An AllostericMechanism keeps those flips, since over more than one catalytic subunit a flank's flip can raise the rank. Flux is decided on the graph of rapid-equilibrium segments (_flux_carrying_groups): each steady-state step is an edge weighted by its net uptake of substrates minus products, segment offsets included, and a step carries flux exactly when its biconnected block holds a cycle of nonzero weight. A steady-state group with no such step exposes only the ratio of its constants, so a flip set that leaves one counts as failed and is extended, and a split part with none is emitted at rapid equilibrium. A dead-end copy of a substrate or product is redundant, and never emitted, when in every conformation where it binds each of its complexes has a productive twin (a form that holds no modifier, in the complex's rapid-equilibrium segment with its offsets, so the two weights are proportional: _productive_twin, tested over a group by _all_twin; every conformation holds its free enzyme, _redundant_copy_groups), and the dwell gauge, which rescales each twin by its own factor and all of the copy's complexes by one factor, is consistent with every kinetic group's shared constants, the copy's own included, an :EqualAI group taking one rescaling in both conformations (_gauge_rescaling, bookkeeping over the edges whose classes, _GaugeClass, are a kinetic group's or a twin form's own, per conformation); a copy whose complexes all have twins but whose gauge fails is kept, since only the gauge proves the copy's constant invisible. The dead-end move skips a redundant placement, the split drops every child that holds a redundant copy group (a split can complete a gauge, and splitting a group that forms a copy's twins can break one, so only the whole child decides), and _expand_change_allo_state and the parent check reject a redundant group (_redundant_copy_groups). A complex that duplicates only a copy's complex never rejects, since the sites that pin either copy may keep both constants separable. expand_mechanisms asserts both rules on every parent (_assert_emission_rules). Both refinement moves share one minimal-set search (_minimal_gaining_sets). Duplicate equations that survive these proofs are collapsed at compile time by eq_hash. A numerical identifiability rank exists only in the test suite, as an oracle for the proofs; nothing in src/ estimates identifiability numerically.

init_mechanisms appends to the seeds their merged and Theorell–Chance variants (_seed_variants, plain Mechanisms only). _merge_isomerization removes a chemistry isomerization and moves every other step at its substrate side onto its product side, so the last substrate's binding becomes fused; _eliminate_form replaces a form whose only two steps are bindings into it by one Theorell–Chance step in a group of its own. Every step of a base starts at rapid equilibrium. The variants are the inclusion-minimal sets of the base's flux-carrying groups (_minimal_flips) whose flip to steady state leaves no rapid-equilibrium turnover cycle (_re_turnover_cycle: each RE step weighted by its uptake of substrates minus products, a turnover cycle being a cycle of nonzero weight), keeps a maximal rate both ways (_has_vmax, condition V), keeps chemistry out of equilibrium with both sides (_chemistry_equilibrates_both_sides, condition C), leaves no bottomless segment and keeps every steady-state group flux-carrying. _seed_candidate_screen runs the first three tests on index arrays with a weighted union-find, so the other two run only on a candidate that passes them. A merged variant whose every merged complex has two steady-state steps, each alone in its group, has the family of the unmerged chain with rapid-equilibrium flanks at the same count and is skipped. _degenerate, the failure of V or C on the active-state graph, marks the starting mechanisms that _base_tier (src/identify_rate_equation.jl) replaces by their flip children that are not degenerate.

Conformational mechanism types declare _requires_hyperbolic_catalysis (true for an AllostericMechanism whose catalytic multiplicity is above 1), and the moves that can give such a mechanism a non-hyperbolic catalytic scheme consult _hyperbolic_catalysis: _expand_to_allosteric promotes a non-hyperbolic parent at multiplicity 1 only, and _expand_re_to_ss filters its emitted children. The predicate scores the rapid-equilibrium segment graph of the catalytic scheme, leaving out every edge that touches a form holding a modifier, a reactant's modifier copy included: for each substrate and product, a directed steady-state edge scores one if it binds the metabolite in that direction plus the count the edge's source form carries beyond its segment's bottom form (the offsets of _segments, which the boundary check's _bottomless_segment also reads), and a segment scores the most any of its forms carries. The equation's degree in the metabolite is the best score over root segments and spanning arborescences toward the root; the predicate decides "at most 1" with reachability checks (_all_reach) rather than a max-arborescence solve, since a weight-2 pattern is one segment or edge scoring 2, a scoring root plus a compatible scoring edge, or two compatible scoring edges. The exactness of the predicate against the derived denominator is pinned by a test over the uni-bi enumeration and the ping-pong-capable bi-bi enumeration, which contains every bi-bi mechanism. A future conformational type (KNF, mnemonic, slow isomerization) adds one _requires_hyperbolic_catalysis method and calls the predicate in its promotion move.

Optimization algorithm architecture

Fitting reaches solvers only through Optimization.jl; the package ships no solver of its own. fit_rate_equation (src/fitting.jl) builds an OptimizationProblem whose objective, _fit_loss, scores each point with loss! through a FitObjective (see Derivation architecture), and calls Optimization.solve with whatever optimizer the caller passes. This gives the package access to the global, gradient-free optimizers that non-convex rate-equation fitting needs — CMA-ES (OptimizationCMAEvolutionStrategy) and BBO differential evolution (OptimizationBBO) are the tested choices — without taking on a solver dependency or locking users into one algorithm. Optimization.jl common options (maxtime, maxiters, …) are named keyword arguments, and solver-specific options pass through a solver_kwargs named tuple; see Loss & optimizers for the user-facing view.
