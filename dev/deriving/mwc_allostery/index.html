Mechanisms with allosteric regulators

Allosteric regulation is one of the main ways metabolic enzymes are controlled. An allosteric regulator binds a regulatory site that is distinct from the catalytic site and, from there, either inhibits or activates the enzyme. A classical description of how this works is the Monod–Wyman–Changeux (MWC) model (Monod et al., 1965), and EnzymeRates can derive rate equations under that model.

In the MWC picture the enzyme exists in two conformations — an active state (A) and an inactive state (I) — in equilibrium with one another. (In the classic MWC notation A is the R state and I is the T state; the package uses A/I throughout, so K_A_… names correspond to the literature's R-state constants.) A coupling constant L sets the inactive-to-active population ratio of the free enzyme. What distinguishes an activator from an inhibitor is simply which conformation it prefers: an activator binds the active state more tightly and shifts the population toward A, while an inhibitor binds the inactive state and shifts it toward I. That preference is expressed entirely through the regulator's binding constants for the two states — its K_A versus its K_I.

Setting up an MWC mechanism

Write it with @enzyme_mechanism, ending each catalytic statement and each regulator with its allosteric state. The printed rate equation shows the result:

using EnzymeRates
allo = @enzyme_mechanism begin
    substrates: S
    products:   P
    oligomeric_state: 2
    steps: begin
        E + S ⇌ ES     :: EqualAI
        ES <--> EP     :: NonequalAI
        EP ⇌ E + P     :: EqualAI
    end
    regulatory_site(2): A::OnlyA
    regulatory_site(2): I::OnlyI
end
print(rate_equation_string(allo))
(; K_EP_to_E_P, K_ES_to_E_S, k_A_ES_to_EP, k_I_ES_to_EP, K_A_Areg, K_I_Ireg, L) = params
(; S, P, A, I) = concs
# Haldane constraints:
k_A_EP_to_ES = K_EP_to_E_P * k_A_ES_to_EP / (K_ES_to_E_S * Keq)
k_I_EP_to_ES = K_EP_to_E_P * k_I_ES_to_EP / (K_ES_to_E_S * Keq)
Q_A = k_A_ES_to_EP / K_ES_to_E_S
D_A = 1 + S / K_ES_to_E_S + P / K_EP_to_E_P
Q_I = k_I_ES_to_EP / K_ES_to_E_S
D_I = 1 + S / K_ES_to_E_S + P / K_EP_to_E_P
W_A = (1 + A / K_A_Areg)^2
W_I = (1 + I / K_I_Ireg)^2
v = (Q_A * D_A * W_A + L * Q_I * D_I * W_I) * (S - P / Keq) / (D_A^2 * W_A + L * D_I^2 * W_I)

The independent parameters the mechanism fits:

parameters(allo)
(:K_EP_to_E_P, :K_ES_to_E_S, :k_A_ES_to_EP, :k_I_ES_to_EP, :K_A_Areg, :K_I_Ireg, :L)

Each catalytic kinetic group and each regulator carries one of four allosteric states, which controls how its A- and I-state symbols are rendered:

StateMeaningExample symbol
:EqualAIOne shared symbol for both statesK_ES_to_E_S
:NonequalAIIndependent A and I symbolsk_A_ES_to_EP, k_I_ES_to_EP
:OnlyAPresent in the active state only; zeroed in the inactive polynomialK_A_Areg
:OnlyIPresent in the inactive state only; zeroed in the active polynomialK_I_Ireg

In the example the two binding steps are :EqualAI, so they share K_ES_to_E_S and K_EP_to_E_P with no A/I token; the isomerization step is :NonequalAI, giving independent k_A_ES_to_EP and k_I_ES_to_EP; the activator A is :OnlyA (K_A_Areg, active state only) and the inhibitor I is :OnlyI (K_I_Ireg, inactive state only). An :EqualAI regulator R would take one bare constant, K_Rreg, in both states. L is the free-enzyme coupling constant. Any catalytic group may be :OnlyI, such as an inhibitor's binding that only the inactive conformation has, or one of two reaction paths, as long as the active conformation still turns substrate into product.

An :OnlyA or :OnlyI state on a catalytic group reaches beyond that group. The conformations interconvert at free E alone, so the conformation that lacks the group also lacks every form its remaining steps no longer join to free E, and the steps between such forms, whatever their own state. If E + S ⇌ ES and EP ⇌ E + P are both :OnlyA, the inactive conformation has neither ES nor EP, and ES <--> EP does not run in it even when it is :EqualAI. One exclusion on a cycle cuts nothing off: the rest of the cycle still joins every form to free E. Allosteric states as limits, and admission says when such a law is accepted.

The mechanism also declares its oligomeric state and the multiplicity of each regulatory site. oligomeric_state is the number of identical catalytic subunits, which change conformation together; it becomes the exponent on each conformation's catalytic partition function. Each regulatory_site(m) says how many copies of that site the oligomer carries, raising the site's binding factor to the power m. In the example both are 2 — a homodimer with two copies of each regulatory site. A regulator is named only at its site: it binds the catalytic site too only where a step takes it up, and is then declared under modifiers: as well.

Note

The rate equation gives the rate per catalytic site: oligomeric_state enters only through the partition-function exponents, never as an overall multiplier on the numerator.

Derivation of the MWC rate equation

The MWC rate equation is assembled from two ordinary single-conformation rate equations — one for the active state, one for the inactive state — combined by a partition function. The derivation rests on two rapid-equilibrium assumptions. Allosteric regulators bind fast relative to catalysis. And the enzyme changes conformation only while free: it settles into the active or inactive state before it binds anything — the free-enzyme inactive-to-active ratio is L — and keeps that conformation for a full catalytic cycle. The overall rate is then each conformation's catalytic cycle weighted by its free-enzyme population. The conformations interconvert only at free E, the form E with nothing bound, and L is its inactive-to-active ratio. A mechanism may hold other free forms, such as a conformation F with F <--> E; they change conformation only by way of E. When another free form sits at rapid equilibrium with E, its own inactive-to-active ratio is L·r_I/r_A, where r_c is the equilibrium ratio [F]/[E] in conformation c across the step that joins F to E, so a fit reports L at E whatever the free forms are named.

An equivalent statement lets every form interconvert between the A and I states, but with a thermodynamically fixed ratio: L for the free enzyme, and L scaled by the form's active-to-inactive binding-constant ratio for a bound form (so L itself for an :EqualAI ligand). The two statements agree except when catalysis differs between the conformations (:NonequalAI); there, committing to one conformation per cycle — the model here — routes each molecule's turnover through a single conformation rather than blending the two, a difference of a few percent.

Treating regulator binding as steady state instead would not help — a regulator is not consumed, so a non-RE binding step contributes only the ratio of its on- and off-rate constants, which is exactly the equilibrium constant the RE treatment already uses, and the two rates would not be separately identifiable from rate data.

For a mechanism with oligomeric state n, the package derives each conformation's law on the graph it keeps (the active conformation without its :OnlyI groups, the inactive one without its :OnlyA groups, each without the forms that dropping those groups cuts off from free E) and combines the two into the rate per catalytic site:

v = (H) * (F_I^n * Q_A * D_A^(n-1) * W_A + L * F_A^n * Q_I * D_I^(n-1) * W_I) /
          (F_I^n * D_A^n * W_A + L * F_A^n * D_I^n * W_I)

H = Π[S] − Π[P]/Keq is the Haldane factor both conformations share. Q_A is the active conformation's numerator over H and D_A its denominator, the King–Altman/Cha polynomials of its catalytic graph; F_A is free E's weight in D_A, the spanning-tree sum of free E's segment times the concentration monomial that brings the law to lowest terms. Q_I, D_I and F_I are the inactive conformation's, with :EqualAI groups keeping the shared constants and :NonequalAI groups their own I names. W_A and W_I multiply the regulatory-site factors (1 + X/K)^m, and L weighs the inactive branch. Each branch carries the other conformation's free-E weight, so both stand on free E, where the conformations interconvert; that keeps a bare rate constant out of the L term.

The printed law lists these polynomials by name. Each conformation's Q, D and F are divided by that conformation's own normalizer, N_A or N_I (Normalization), which the combination does not see. Every term of D_A and D_I is then dimensionless, except in a conformation with no normalizer, which prints its polynomials as derived. F_A and F_I print only when they differ. They are 1 whenever each conformation's denominator has a constant term and the graph it keeps is one rapid-equilibrium segment, with E its only free form and no covalent form. That is the common case, and the law is then the textbook MWC form. When the :OnlyA groups stop every catalytic cycle of the inactive conformation, as the search's allosteric bases do, the law has no Q_I: the inactive branch adds enzyme to the denominator and no flux. When the inactive conformation is free E alone, the law has no D_I either, and its branch of the denominator is L times F_A^n and W_I, each left out when it is 1. With coefficients = true each polynomial reads in named coefficients (dA_S for the coefficient of S in D_A, nI_0 for the constant term of Q_I).

In the printed equation above n = 2, so each D is squared in the denominator, the activator's site gives W_A = (1 + A / K_A_Areg)^2, and the inhibitor's W_I = (1 + I / K_I_Ireg)^2.

The term cap counts both conformations: a law whose two conformations hold more than 5,000 spanning-tree products together is refused, and identify's eq_complexity_filter compares the same sum.

Allosteric states as limits, and admission

An :OnlyA state excludes a group's steps from the inactive conformation, and :OnlyI excludes them from the active one. The derivation reads each conformation's law by deleting the steps it excludes and the forms that the deletion cuts off from free E. Admission decides whether that law is the limit of ordinary mass-action kinetics as the excluded steps vanish, each at its own rate. When it cannot show that it is, it rejects the states with an error that says why and names the steps at fault where it has them.

How an excluded step is switched off

The package reads an excluded step as nearly absent, not absent: in the conformation that excludes it, the step has a very high dissociation constant or very low rate constants, and every thermodynamic constraint, each Haldane and Wegscheider relation, still holds in each conformation.

A step keeps its kind in both conformations: a rapid-equilibrium step stays at rapid equilibrium where it is excluded, and a steady-state step stays at steady state. Rapid equilibrium and steady state differ more than 100-fold in speed, and turning a fast step into the slowest step of the other conformation is not a reasonable approximation. A mechanism that needs it is written with that step steady-state.

The picture is one of energies. In each conformation the steps it keeps join the forms into islands: sets of forms joined to each other by those steps. An island is neither a kinetic group nor a rapid-equilibrium segment. Free E's island holds the forms of that conformation's law. Every other island is cut off: it lies higher in energy than free E's island and holds no enzyme. An excluded step is switched off according to where it lies:

Excluded stepHow it is switched off
Between two islandsIt loses its rate uphill, into the higher island, and keeps its rate downhill. A binding whose bound side lies higher loses its affinity: its dissociation constant grows without bound. A rapid-equilibrium step stays fast.
Between two islands, steady stateIt may stop instead: both rate constants fall to zero.
Inside one island, steady stateIt stops: both rate constants fall to zero, their ratio fixed by thermodynamics.
Inside one island, rapid equilibriumError. Its two sides lie at one energy, so thermodynamics fixes its equilibrium constant, and it could be switched off only by turning slow.

The mechanism fixes the islands but not which lies above which. The package looks for a choice of energies, and of the steady-state steps that stop, under which it can show that the law is the limit, and accepts the mechanism when it finds one. When the excluded steps allow too many choices to search, it tries only three, and rejects the mechanism when it can show the limit under none of them. The choice does not change the law, which leaves the excluded steps out. The package takes the law to be the limit under a choice when:

  1. Existence. The steps that lose a rate, each drawn uphill, close no cycle and never point into free E's island, so the energies exist and no island lies below free E's.
  2. Uniqueness. They lead from free E's island to every cut-off island, so each cut-off island empties. On every face where data sit (below), the steady state does not depend on where the enzyme starts, no cut-off form fills faster than it empties, and no excluded step opens a faster route between two forms of the law, unless the cut-off forms on that route cannot change the limit.
  3. Turnover. With :OnlyI groups, the active conformation still turns the reaction over.

Four examples follow, each excluding steps from the inactive conformation. In the first, the substrate binding and the catalytic step are excluded:

both_excluded = @enzyme_mechanism begin
    substrates: S
    products:   P
    steps: begin
        E + S ⇌ ES     :: OnlyA
        ES <--> EP     :: OnlyA
        EP ⇌ E + P     :: EqualAI
    end
end
print(rate_equation_string(both_excluded))
(; K_A_ES_to_E_S, K_EP_to_E_P, k_A_ES_to_EP, L) = params
(; S, P) = concs
# Haldane constraints:
k_A_EP_to_ES = K_EP_to_E_P * k_A_ES_to_EP / (K_A_ES_to_E_S * Keq)
Q_A = k_A_ES_to_EP / K_A_ES_to_E_S
D_A = 1 + S / K_A_ES_to_E_S + P / K_EP_to_E_P
D_I = 1 + P / K_EP_to_E_P
v = Q_A * (S - P / Keq) / (D_A + L * D_I)

In the inactive conformation only the product binding runs, so E and EP form free E's island and ES is cut off. The substrate binding loses its affinity, and the catalytic step stops or loses its rate into ES. The inactive conformation binds P and turns nothing over: the law has D_I and no Q_I.

With the substrate binding excluded alone, the package rejects the mechanism:

The catalytic step and the product binding still join ES to free E, so ES stays in free E's island, made from P, and the substrate binding lies inside one island. Keq and the other steps' constants fix its dissociation constant, which cannot grow.

The same binding at steady state is accepted:

ss_excluded = @enzyme_mechanism begin
    substrates: S
    products:   P
    steps: begin
        E + S <--> ES  :: OnlyA
        ES <--> EP     :: EqualAI
        EP ⇌ E + P     :: EqualAI
    end
end;
@enzyme_mechanism begin
    substrates: S
    products: P
    oligomeric_state: 1

        E + P ⇌ EP :: EqualAI

end

The substrate binding again lies inside free E's island, and at steady state it stops. The inactive conformation keeps ES, made from P, and turns nothing over. identify_rate_equation never proposes such a mechanism, in which a conformation keeps a form that only excluded bindings enter, but you may write one.

The last example is a ping-pong whose inactive conformation runs neither half-reaction. Write F for the modified enzyme E_res_plusA_minusP, and FP and FB for its forms with P and B bound, EP_res_plusA_minusP and EB_res_plusA_minusP:

pingpong = @enzyme_mechanism begin
    substrates: A, B
    products:   P, Q
    steps: begin
        E + A ⇌ EA                                     :: EqualAI
        EA <--> EP_res_plusA_minusP                    :: OnlyA
        EP_res_plusA_minusP ⇌ E_res_plusA_minusP + P   :: EqualAI
        E_res_plusA_minusP + B ⇌ EB_res_plusA_minusP   :: EqualAI
        EB_res_plusA_minusP ⇌ EQ                       :: OnlyA
        EQ ⇌ E + Q                                     :: EqualAI
    end
end;
@enzyme_mechanism begin
    substrates: A, B
    products: P, Q
    oligomeric_state: 1
    steps: begin
        E + A ⇌ EA :: EqualAI
        E + Q ⇌ EQ :: EqualAI
        EA <--> EP_res_plusA_minusP :: OnlyA
        EB_res_plusA_minusP ⇌ EQ :: OnlyA
        E_res_plusA_minusP + B ⇌ EB_res_plusA_minusP :: EqualAI
        E_res_plusA_minusP + P ⇌ EP_res_plusA_minusP :: EqualAI
    end
end

In the inactive conformation E, EA and EQ form free E's island, and F, FP and FB form one cut-off island. Each half-reaction runs between the two islands: the first loses its rate from EA into FP, the second its rate from EQ into FB, and each keeps its rate back. The modified enzyme then empties through both halves, whatever the ratio of the two vanishing rates, and the inactive conformation binds A and Q and turns nothing over.

Two of the face checks, that no cut-off form fills faster than it empties and that the cut-off forms on a faster route cannot change the limit, rest on numerical tests, not on a proof. The package was run on 78,554 mechanisms. Among those it accepts only through its search for energies or through cut-off forms that cannot change the limit, two numerical oracles agree with it on every mechanism they checked; Well-posedness guard, on the Developer page, gives the evidence. Admission is not exact: it rejects some mechanisms whose law is the limit, which the repository's ROADMAP.md lists.

When the steps a conformation keeps join the two sides of an excluded rapid-equilibrium step, as in the second example, the error says so and offers each fix that the package accepts: make the step's kinetic group steady-state, so that it stops (the third example), or exclude the steps that keep its two sides joined, here the product binding and the catalytic step, so that one side is cut off. In the active conformation that fix makes them :OnlyI.

What the package rejects

A mechanism is well defined when its rate law is the unique fast-equilibrium limit of mass-action kinetics wherever data sit: at interior concentrations and on every face, where every product is zero, every substrate, each reactant alone on a side of two or more, or any of these with every modifier-only name zero. @enzyme_mechanism throws an error that states the fault and names the steps at fault and the face where it has them, and the mechanism search drops the mechanism. The package rejects:

  1. an unbalanced cycle: a cycle of steps that takes up or gives off a metabolite in another proportion than the reaction, as when a step leaves out a metabolite;
  2. no catalytic cycle: no cycle of steps turns substrates into products, so the rate is zero;
  3. a cycle of rapid-equilibrium steps that turns the reaction over, so the rate is infinite;
  4. a face where the law is not the unique limit: a rapid-equilibrium segment with no form whose weight keeps the segment's lowest power of each of the face's metabolites, a segment whose lowest forms are joined only through other forms, a trap that the face feeds and its fast exits cannot drain, a second closed set of forms, or a law with no value (0/0) there;
  5. allosteric states for which the package finds no choice of energies under which it can show that the law is the limit. When the states allow too many choices to search, it tries only three (admission, above).

The faces leave out the corners that zero one substrate with every product. A law the package accepts can be 0/0 there, where a well-defined law is 0, so the compiled rate equation returns NaN at such a point; a fit scores the NaN as it scores the 0 a well-defined law predicts there.

Thermodynamic constraints of MWC equations

A single conformation already carries thermodynamic constraints: every cycle of steps must agree with the overall equilibrium constant Keq (a Haldane relation), and every cycle of binding steps must be path-independent (a Wegscheider relation). The derivation reports both and expresses the dependent rate constants through the independent ones. An MWC equation solves the relations of both conformations together, adding constraints neither has alone. Here we review several examples of such additional constraints.

Make a lone substrate binding :NonequalAI while catalysis and product release stay :EqualAI, and the split collapses:

collapse = @enzyme_mechanism begin
    substrates: S
    products:   P
    oligomeric_state: 2
    steps: begin
        E + S ⇌ ES     :: NonequalAI
        ES <--> EP     :: EqualAI
        EP ⇌ E + P     :: EqualAI
    end
    regulatory_site(2): R::OnlyI
end
print(rate_equation_string(collapse))
(; K_A_ES_to_E_S, K_EP_to_E_P, k_ES_to_EP, K_I_Rreg, L) = params
(; S, P, R) = concs
# Wegscheider constraints:
K_I_ES_to_E_S = K_A_ES_to_E_S
# Haldane constraints:
k_EP_to_ES = K_EP_to_E_P * k_ES_to_EP / (K_A_ES_to_E_S * Keq)
Q_A = k_ES_to_EP / K_A_ES_to_E_S
D_A = 1 + S / K_A_ES_to_E_S + P / K_EP_to_E_P
Q_I = k_ES_to_EP / K_A_ES_to_E_S
D_I = 1 + S / K_A_ES_to_E_S + P / K_EP_to_E_P
W_I = (1 + R / K_I_Rreg)^2
v = (Q_A * D_A + L * Q_I * D_I * W_I) * (S - P / Keq) / (D_A^2 + L * D_I^2 * W_I)

The equation reports K_I_ES_to_E_S = K_A_ES_to_E_S. The Haldane relation fixes Keq from the catalytic rate constants and the two affinities in each conformation; with catalysis, product release, and Keq all shared, the substrate affinity is pinned as well. The state asked for a difference thermodynamics forbids, so the substrate stops distinguishing the conformations.

Make both the substrate and product bindings :NonequalAI, and one degree of freedom survives:

coupled = @enzyme_mechanism begin
    substrates: S
    products:   P
    oligomeric_state: 2
    steps: begin
        E + S ⇌ ES     :: NonequalAI
        ES <--> EP     :: EqualAI
        EP ⇌ E + P     :: NonequalAI
    end
    regulatory_site(2): R::OnlyI
end
print(rate_equation_string(coupled))
(; K_A_EP_to_E_P, K_A_ES_to_E_S, K_I_EP_to_E_P, k_ES_to_EP, K_I_Rreg, L) = params
(; S, P, R) = concs
# Wegscheider constraints:
K_I_ES_to_E_S = K_A_ES_to_E_S * K_I_EP_to_E_P / K_A_EP_to_E_P
# Haldane constraints:
k_EP_to_ES = K_A_EP_to_E_P * k_ES_to_EP / (K_A_ES_to_E_S * Keq)
Q_A = k_ES_to_EP / K_A_ES_to_E_S
D_A = 1 + S / K_A_ES_to_E_S + P / K_A_EP_to_E_P
Q_I = K_A_EP_to_E_P * k_ES_to_EP / (K_A_ES_to_E_S * K_I_EP_to_E_P)
D_I = 1 + S * K_A_EP_to_E_P / (K_A_ES_to_E_S * K_I_EP_to_E_P) + P / K_I_EP_to_E_P
W_I = (1 + R / K_I_Rreg)^2
v = (Q_A * D_A + L * Q_I * D_I * W_I) * (S - P / Keq) / (D_A^2 + L * D_I^2 * W_I)

Now K_I_ES_to_E_S = K_A_ES_to_E_S · K_I_EP_to_E_P / K_A_EP_to_E_P, i.e. K_S^I / K_S^A = K_P^I / K_P^A: the affinities differ freely as long as they differ together, holding their ratio fixed. This is the thermodynamically consistent form of a K-system, and it needs two coupled :NonequalAI bindings, not one.

Often a K-system uses exclusive :OnlyA binding: the substrate binds the active state alone, and the inactive cycle is pruned (no Q_I). When that binding is at rapid equilibrium, its catalytic step must be :OnlyA too. This is a thermodynamic requirement, not a convention: with :EqualAI or :NonequalAI catalysis and an :EqualAI product binding, the inactive conformation still joins E to ES through catalysis and product release, so thermodynamics fixes the substrate affinity there, and the package rejects the combination (How an excluded step is switched off). A steady-state substrate binding is stopped instead: the inactive conformation keeps ES, made from the product, and turns nothing over.

The exception is a balanced K-system. When :OnlyA binds both a substrate and a product, ES and EP form one cut-off island in the inactive conformation, which rises as a whole: the two K_I → ∞ limits cancel in the Haldane relation, the affinities diverge together and their ratio stays free, so :EqualAI catalysis is legal.

A steady-state binding splits the constraint further, carrying an affinity (a ratio of its two rate constants) the cycles constrain and a speed (their product) they do not: a forbidden affinity collapses while the speed stays free, so the two conformations bind with the same Kd but different kinetics. Making the substrate binding steady-state and :NonequalAI derives its reverse rate (k_I_ES_to_E_S = k_A_ES_to_E_S · k_I_E_S_to_ES / k_A_E_S_to_ES) and leaves the forward rate a free parameter:

ss = @enzyme_mechanism begin
    substrates: S
    products:   P
    oligomeric_state: 2
    steps: begin
        E + S <--> ES  :: NonequalAI
        ES <--> EP     :: EqualAI
        EP ⇌ E + P     :: EqualAI
    end
    regulatory_site(2): R::OnlyI
end
parameters(ss)
(:K_EP_to_E_P, :k_A_ES_to_E_S, :k_A_E_S_to_ES, :k_ES_to_EP, :k_I_E_S_to_ES, :K_I_Rreg, :L)
