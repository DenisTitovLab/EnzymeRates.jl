Thermodynamic constraints

Every reversible mechanism contains cycles: sequences of steps that return the enzyme to its starting form. Thermodynamics constrains the rate constants around each cycle — their product must equal a fixed function of the overall equilibrium constant. The package identifies these constraints automatically and removes one rate constant per independent cycle, reducing the number of fitted parameters and guaranteeing that every fitted equation is thermodynamically consistent.

Haldane and Wegscheider relations

Two kinds of constraint arise (Haldane, 1930) (Wegscheider, 1902), one for each kind of cycle.

A Haldane constraint comes from a cycle whose net change matches the overall reaction — there is one per independent cycle that turns substrate into product. Its dependent rate constant is a power product that carries Keq, so the forward and reverse rates around the cycle cannot be chosen independently of the equilibrium constant. For the reversible Michaelis–Menten mechanism the reverse catalytic rate is fixed by Keq, the two binding constants, and the forward rate:

k_EP_to_ES = (1 / Keq) * K_EP_to_E_P * (1 / K_ES_to_E_S) * k_ES_to_EP

A Wegscheider constraint comes from a cycle that closes on itself with zero net metabolite change — one per independent internal loop. Its dependent rate constant is a power product of the others with no Keq factor. These appear when the same enzyme form can be reached by more than one binding order: detailed balance forces the binding constants around the loop to multiply consistently. For a random-order mechanism in which substrates A and B can bind in either order, the four binding constants are tied:

K_EA_to_E_A * K_EAB_to_EA_B = K_EB_to_E_B * K_EAB_to_EB_A

When the other constants of a loop cancel, the loop fixes the remaining one at 1 and its line reads K_E_to_F = 1.0. An inhibitor that binds conformations E and F into one complex E(I) (E + I ⇌ E(I) and F + I ⇌ E(I)) under one shared dissociation constant does this: the loop E → E(I) → F → E forces E and F to equal weight.

How the constraints are found

The package takes the fundamental cycles of a spanning forest of the enzyme-form graph, one per step outside the forest, so the cycle rows come out linearly independent by construction. Each cycle is classified by its net metabolite change: proportional to the overall reaction makes it a Haldane cycle, zero makes it a Wegscheider cycle. An allosteric mechanism writes every cycle once per conformation, and the steps of a kinetic group add rows that tie their constants together. One exact rational elimination over all of these rows chooses the dependent constants (integer arithmetic that switches to big integers if a value grows too large), so the same constraints come out every time. If a mechanism is thermodynamically infeasible — its rows combine to 0 = c · log(Keq) with c ≠ 0 — the package raises an error rather than emitting a silent or wrong equation.

The choice of independent vs dependent parameters

Each independent cycle costs one constant. The package decides which constant becomes dependent by a fixed priority, read in turn; the earlier a constant ranks, the sooner it becomes dependent:

  1. Conformation. In an allosteric mechanism, the inactive conformation's own constants go before the active ones.
  2. Kind of step. Isomerizations (steps that exchange no metabolite, on-enzyme chemistry included) go first, then other chemistry (a binding that also converts, a Theorell–Chance step), then plain bindings.
  3. Occupancy. Steps whose two enzyme forms both hold a metabolite go before steps that touch free E or a form that holds only a covalent residual.
  4. Speed. Steady-state constants go before rapid-equilibrium ones.
  5. Direction. Within a steady-state step, the rate constant that runs against the reaction goes first: the reverse rate, except that for the binding of a product the uptake runs against the reaction. So in E(S) <--> E + P, stored as the binding E + P → E(S), the product uptake is dependent and the catalytic rate stays fitted.

The free-enzyme binding constants a biochemist measures therefore stay independent whenever a cycle leaves them free. Ties go to a content key built from the forms and metabolites of each step, never from the order in which steps were written, so two mechanisms that differ only in step order, step direction or how the kinetic groups were labelled have the same dependents. A kinetic group's constant takes the name of the step the solve keeps, so splitting a group whose parts the cycles already tie changes no fitted name. The key reads metabolite names, though: renaming a metabolite can change which of two tied constants is dependent.

A concrete example

A random-order mechanism — two substrates that can bind in either order — carries both kinds of constraint at once: a Wegscheider tie among its binding constants and a Haldane tie on its catalytic step.

using EnzymeRates
m = @enzyme_mechanism begin
    substrates: A, B
    products:   P
    steps: begin
        E + A ⇌ E(A)
        E + B ⇌ E(B)
        E(A) + B ⇌ E(A, B)
        E(B) + A ⇌ E(A, B)
        E(A, B) <--> E(P)
        E(P) ⇌ E + P
    end
end
print(rate_equation_string(m))
(; K_EAB_to_EA_B, K_EA_to_E_A, K_EB_to_E_B, K_EP_to_E_P, k_EAB_to_EP, Keq, E_total) = params
(; A, B, P) = concs
# Wegscheider constraints:
K_EAB_to_EB_A = K_EAB_to_EA_B * K_EA_to_E_A * (1 / K_EB_to_E_B)
# Haldane constraints:
k_EP_to_EAB = (1 / Keq) * (1 / K_EAB_to_EA_B) * (1 / K_EA_to_E_A) * K_EP_to_E_P * k_EAB_to_EP
v = E_total * (k_EAB_to_EP * A * B / (K_EAB_to_EA_B * K_EA_to_E_A) - k_EP_to_EAB * P / K_EP_to_E_P) / (1 + A / K_EA_to_E_A + B / K_EB_to_E_B + P / K_EP_to_E_P + A * B / (K_EAB_to_EA_B * K_EA_to_E_A))

The # Wegscheider constraints: section ties the binding constants of the two orders together (no Keq), and the # Haldane constraints: section fixes the reverse catalytic rate from Keq and the forward constants. Two constants are therefore dependent and do not appear in the fitted-parameter list:

parameters(m)
(:K_EAB_to_EA_B, :K_EA_to_E_A, :K_EB_to_E_B, :K_EP_to_E_P, :k_EAB_to_EP, :Keq, :E_total)

Both eliminated constants are absent from parameters(m), so the fit explores only the thermodynamically independent directions — every candidate equation the fitter evaluates already satisfies detailed balance.
