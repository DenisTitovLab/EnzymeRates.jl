Derivation tutorial

This tutorial shows how to define the reversible Michaelis–Menten mechanism and derive its rate equation as a String. After reading it, you will recognize the package's parameter names as the textbook constants you already know.

Defining the mechanism

Use @enzyme_mechanism to declare the mechanism. The ⇌ arrow marks a step as rapid equilibrium (RE); <--> marks it as steady state (SS).

julia> using EnzymeRates

julia> m = @enzyme_mechanism begin
           substrates: S
           products:   P
           steps: begin
               E + S ⇌ E(S)
               E(S) <--> E(P)
               E(P) ⇌ E + P
           end
       end;

julia> m isa EnzymeMechanism
true

The two binding steps are rapid equilibrium; the central isomerization E(S) <--> E(P) is steady state. That single SS step is the rate-limiting catalytic step.

Deriving the rate equation

rate_equation_string returns the symbolic rate equation as a multi-line String (it does not print). Call print to display it without escaped newlines:

julia> print(rate_equation_string(m))
(; K_EP_to_E_P, K_ES_to_E_S, k_ES_to_EP, Keq, E_total) = params
(; S, P) = concs
# Haldane constraints:
k_EP_to_ES = K_EP_to_E_P * k_ES_to_EP / (K_ES_to_E_S * Keq)
v = E_total * (k_ES_to_EP / K_ES_to_E_S) * (S - P / Keq) / (1 + S / K_ES_to_E_S + P / K_EP_to_E_P)

The string reads, in order:

  1. a params destructuring line: the independent fitted constants, then Keq and E_total;
  2. a concs destructuring line: the concentrations;
  3. the reference lines under # Haldane constraints: (after # Wegscheider constraints: when a cycle of bindings ties constants together): each dependent constant in Keq and the independent constants, for reference, since the law reads only the independent ones;
  4. when the normalizer has a factor that is not a single term, or an integer to a fractional power, the line N = … (Normalization);
  5. the law, v = E_total * (Q) * (H) / (D). H = S - P / Keq vanishes at equilibrium, and Q and D have positive terms.

Normalization

Every term of D is dimensionless unless the law has no normalizer (rule 4). A unit here counts each concentration as a dimension of its own. The package divides numerator and denominator by a normalizer with D's unit, chosen by the first rule that applies.

  1. Constant term. When D has a term free of concentrations, divide by that term's coefficient, the part of D free of concentrations; every term is then dimensionless. In a rapid-equilibrium law whose only form that binds nothing is free E, as in 1 + S / K_ES_to_E_S + P / K_EP_to_E_P above, that part is free E's weight, and D sums the enzyme forms' abundances relative to free E.
  2. Cleland's normalizer. When D holds the term ΠS/X for every substrate X and the term ΠS, ΠS the product of the substrates, divide by N = Π_X c(ΠS/X) / c(ΠS)^(n−1), c(m) the coefficient of the term m and n the number of substrates. Each substrate term then reads as its concentrations over their Michaelis constants, A·B·C/(K_a·K_b·K_c). The ping-pong bi-bi of Ping-pong mechanisms takes this rule.
  3. Virtual constant term. Otherwise take D's terms in a fixed order (lowest total degree first, then fewest metabolites other than substrates, then metabolite order) and weigh the first affinely independent ones so that the product of their coefficients, each to its weight, has D's unit. Fractional weights print as roots.
  4. No normalizer. When no such weights exist, no product of the coefficients has D's unit: some weighting of the concentrations gives every term of D the same nonzero degree. In E + A <--> E(A), E(A) + B ⇌ E(Q) + P, E(Q) <--> E + Q, for example, the rapid-equilibrium Theorell–Chance step makes every term carry exactly one of B and P. The law then prints as derived, and its terms keep their units.

A factor of the normalizer that is not a single term, or an integer to a fractional power, prints once, as the line N = …, and the terms it divides read / N.

The coefficient form

coefficients = true prints the same law with one name per coefficient. It too runs from top to bottom: the params and concs lines, a comment line, N when it prints, one line per coefficient in the fitted constants, for an allosteric law its polynomials in those coefficients and its site factors, then v. The reference lines print only in the full law.

julia> print(rate_equation_string(m; coefficients = true))
(; K_EP_to_E_P, K_ES_to_E_S, k_ES_to_EP, Keq, E_total) = params
(; S, P) = concs
# The coefficients are functions of the fitted constants, not parameters:
n_0 = k_ES_to_EP / K_ES_to_E_S
d_S = 1 / K_ES_to_E_S
d_P = 1 / K_EP_to_E_P
v = E_total * n_0 * (S - P / Keq) / (1 + d_S * S + d_P * P)

A coefficient is named by the concentrations it multiplies: d_A_B for A * B and d_A²_B for A^2 * B; n_0 is the numerator's constant term. The fit still runs over the fitted constants.

Meaning of parameter names

Package symbolRoleUnits
K_ES_to_E_SDissociation constant of substrate S from the E–S complex. For a one-substrate, one-product Michaelis–Menten enzyme it is analogous to the Michaelis constant Km under the rapid-equilibrium approximation, but it is not the same as Km for more complex mechanisms.M
K_EP_to_E_PDissociation constant of product P from the E–P complex.M
k_ES_to_EPRate of conversion of enzyme species ES to enzyme species EP.1/s
k_EP_to_ESRate of the reverse conversion, EP to ES.1/s
k_E_S_to_ESRate constant for binding of S to E.1/(s·M)
k_ES_to_E_SRate of dissociation of S from the E–S complex.1/s
KeqEquilibrium constant of the overall reaction; always user-supplied.unitless
E_totalActive-site (protomer) concentration. For a monomer this equals the total enzyme concentration; for an allosteric enzyme with N catalytic subunits it is N × the oligomer concentration, so allosteric kcat is reported per active site.M

Inspecting parameters and metabolites

The @example block below shows the fitted-parameter tuple and the concentration symbols returned by parameters and metabolites:

using EnzymeRates
m = @enzyme_mechanism begin
    substrates: S
    products:   P
    steps: begin
        E + S ⇌ E(S)
        E(S) <--> E(P)
        E(P) ⇌ E + P
    end
end
(parameters(m), metabolites(m))
((:K_EP_to_E_P, :K_ES_to_E_S, :k_ES_to_EP, :Keq, :E_total), (:S, :P))

parameters(m) returns the symbols the fitter expects: the three independent constants plus Keq and E_total. metabolites(m) returns the concentration symbols the rate equation reads at runtime.

Every constant of the rate law

all_parameters adds the dependent constants to a set of independent values, each computed from its Haldane or Wegscheider relation:

all_parameters(m, (K_EP_to_E_P = 1.0, K_ES_to_E_S = 2.0, k_ES_to_EP = 3.0, Keq = 4.0))
(K_EP_to_E_P = 1.0, K_ES_to_E_S = 2.0, k_ES_to_EP = 3.0, Keq = 4.0, k_EP_to_ES = 0.375)

Here the Haldane relation fixes the reverse catalytic rate k_EP_to_ES from Keq, the two binding constants and the forward rate. See Rapid equilibrium vs steady state for the contrast between RE and SS parameters, and how adding SS steps changes the parameter list.
